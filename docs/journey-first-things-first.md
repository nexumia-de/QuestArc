# Journey: First Things First

Issue #76. The owner asked for a journey for work, to be more productive,
and chose the name and the focus durations (15 → 45 minutes) on
2026-11-06. It was written from [Writing a journey](authoring-journeys.md)
alone, as a test of the guide. File:
[`content/journeys/first-things-first.json`](../content/journeys/first-things-first.json).
Evidence: [focused work](research.md#focused-work-evidence-for-the-journey-first-things-first).

## Stages

All stages on Monday to Friday; anchor "sitting down at my desk".

| Stage        | Plan ("After sitting down at my desk, I will …")                                         | Condition    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| `one-thing`  | write down the one task that matters most today                                          | 4 of 5 days  |
| `focus-15`   | write down my one task and give it 15 quiet minutes, notifications off                   | 4 of 5 days  |
| `focus-45`   | write down my one task and give it 45 quiet minutes, notifications off                   | 4 of 5 days  |
| `focus-mail` | write down my one task, give it 45 quiet minutes, then check email and chat in one block | 8 of 10 days |
| `look-back`  | note what moved forward yesterday, write down my one task and give it 45 quiet minutes   | 8 of 10 days |

The journey builds a **Crossing**
([game rules](game-rules.md) §7), with lanterns on the marker (the ladder's default): the
journey is about getting across to the one task before the stream of
email and chat, and each stage makes that way over sturdier, from
stepping stones to a covered bridge. Five stages need six versions and
the ladder has five, so the covered bridge gains one fixed addition for
the last stage (the checker's warning is expected). The checker also
warns that the second stage asks for 15 minutes: a user who skips the
first stage (#81) starts there; whether to put a smaller stage in
between is open. Home was the other
candidate; a home fits a morning routine better, and the two built-in
journeys then build different things.

## How it follows the evidence and the design rules

- **Start tiny** (rule 1): writing down one task takes a minute on the
  busiest day, and every later stage still starts with it, so it is the
  fallback on hard days (rule 11).
- **If-then plan with one anchor** (rule 2): sitting down at the desk is
  a stable cue on workdays; the plan is an implementation intention, the
  best-supported part (with a small effect).
- **One task before messages:** task-switching costs and attention
  residue support doing the one task first and checking email and chat
  afterwards, in one block. Batching email lowered stress in one
  experiment; it did not raise productivity.
- **Look back:** one line on yesterday's progress is self-monitoring
  (causal evidence) and fits the small-wins diary study (correlational)
  and the reflection experiments (working paper).
- **Honest texts** (rules 4, 15, 26): no promise of more output, no
  minutes to refocus, no Pomodoro or 90-minute cycles; the durations
  are a ramp the user may change. There are no opponents (version 3,
  #110); the texts speak of quiet that is built on what is already
  there.
- **Nuggets** (rule 22): a pen and sticky notes where you sit down; why
  one missed workday is fine; a parking list for thoughts that come up;
  an if-then plan for a day full of meetings; why the one line helps.
- The action is also the station's name in the list of stations, cut after two
  lines, so the part that differs from the previous stage comes early
  ("15" or "45 quiet minutes", "then check email").
