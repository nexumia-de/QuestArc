# Repetition, not rewards, builds QuestArc's habits

The science supports QuestArc's calm core far more than its game layer. Habits form through **repeated performance of one small action in a stable context**. Automaticity rises along a curve that took a **median of 59–66 days in trials but ranged from 4 to 335 days** across individuals ([Singh et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/)). **A single missed day did not materially hurt habit formation**, while very inconsistent performance did ([BPS on Lally 2010](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)). The best-evidenced levers QuestArc already uses or can add cheaply are these: if-then plans tied to an existing routine, self-monitoring with feedback and goal review, autonomy-supportive design, tolerance of single lapses, a comeback acknowledgement after a miss, and graduation once the habit runs without the app. The game elements (XP, levels, guardians, narrative) have **small, heterogeneous and fading effects** (behavioural g ≈ 0.25 in learning contexts; g ≈ 0.42 for physical activity, both fragile). They are worth keeping as an optional, deterministic, non-punitive layer, but QuestArc should not present them as the mechanism of change. Reminders help repetition but **can hinder automaticity** because the phone becomes the cue, so they should restate the user's own plan and fade. Generic motivational quotes have **no experimental evidence of changing behaviour**. Short, actionable learning nuggets have modest support from a habit-advice RCT and from message-intervention meta-analyses. A better replacement for quotes is the user's own "why", backed by a self-affirmation meta-analysis (behaviour d = 0.32). Several effects the app could lean on rest on single, unreplicated papers: goal gradient, endowed progress, implementation-intention overload, the what-the-hell effect and self-compassion. The biggest gap is QuestArc's own signature feature: no study tests what happens when tracking is removed after a habit becomes automatic.

**How to read this report.** Each technique is given as _claim → evidence strength → replication status → recommendation (use / add / avoid) and how_. Strength labels are MA (meta-analysis or systematic review), Mega (megastudy), RCT, Field exp., Single study, Diary/Correlational, and Popular claim. Every citation keeps the verification marking from the research notes. **[verified]** means a researcher confirmed the source or figure in the research session from a publisher, PubMed, repository or abstract page. **[unverified]** means a DOI, figure or detail came from prior knowledge or a secondary source and was not checked against the primary text. Note that **www.nature.com was unreachable** from the research environment (DNS failure). All figures for the Milkman et al. 2021 gym megastudy therefore come from the Caltech record, Penn Today, a Scimex/Springer Nature summary, CNN and a Berkeley abstract, not from the paper itself.

## Habits form in two to five months, and one miss does not break them

**Time to automaticity.** _Claim:_ the "21 days" rule is false, and formation time varies widely. _Evidence:_ Lally et al. 2010 is a single observational study (n = 96 recruited, 82 completed, 84 days), DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674 [verified]. In it, the time to 95% of the automaticity plateau had a median of 66 days (range 18–254). That median comes only from the roughly 39 participants whose data fitted the curve, and the upper range is extrapolated beyond the observation window ([The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit); [UCL 2009](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). Keller et al. 2021 is an RCT (n = 192), DOI 10.1111/bjhp.12504 [verified]. It found a **median of 59 days to peak automaticity** among people who formed habits ([PubMed 33405284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33405284/); [Refubium](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/handle/fub188/31524)). Only **23% reached the study's predefined habit threshold**; that figure comes from a secondary source and is [unverified] ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-habit-systematic-review-59-66-335-days/)). Singh et al. 2024 is an MA, DOI 10.3390/healthcare12232488 [verified]. It pooled the four studies that reported formation time: medians 59–66 days, means 106–154 days, individual range 4–335 days. It also found that morning and self-selected habits were stronger ([PubMed 39685110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250124/Healthy-habits-take-longer-to-form-than-expected.aspx)). Buyalskaya et al. 2023 is a large observational study (more than 12 million gym check-ins and more than 40 million handwashing observations), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2216115120 [verified]. It found that **handwashing became predictable in weeks and gym going in months**. The strongest predictor of attendance was **time since the last visit**, and habitual gym-goers responded less to incentives ([PMC10151500](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/04/230417155750.htm)). _Replication:_ the range of findings converges across studies, but no single figure replicates. A multi-centre replication of Lally was proposed as a registered report, and its publication status was not verified ([PCI RR preprint](https://rr.peercommunityin.org/download/t_recommendations.reply_pdf.8382af6b73bdd0f7.6d616e757363726970745f7072657072696e742e706466.pdf)). _Recommendation — use:_ never promise a day count. Copy should say "most simple habits take about two to five months, and it varies a lot". Prefer daily cadence and self-chosen habits for early stages.

**Missing once.** _Claim:_ one missed opportunity does not derail formation, but inconsistency does. _Evidence:_ Lally 2010 (single study) [verified] ([BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit); [UCL blog](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/bsh/2012/06/29); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/70653523.pdf)). Buyalskaya 2023 supports it: risk rises with the length of the gap. _Replication:_ this rests on few missed-day data points and has not been independently replicated. _Recommendation — use_ "never miss twice", but describe it honestly as a heuristic. No study compares tolerating zero, one or two consecutive misses.

**Measuring automaticity.** _Claim:_ automaticity can be measured with the 4-item Self-Report Behavioural Automaticity Index (SRBAI), the automaticity subscale of the Self-Report Habit Index. _Evidence:_ Gardner et al. 2012 showed the SRBAI is reliable and predictive, DOI 10.1186/1479-5868-9-102 [verified] ([PMC3552971](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3552971/)). The exact item wording is from memory and needs a check [unverified]. A 2024 secondary analysis by Gardner, Lally & Rebar of 16 datasets reportedly found that the single item "I do this automatically" best replicated the full scale. Its citation details are [unverified]. _Replication:_ the SRBAI is widely used, but **no validated cut-off for "habit formed" exists**. _Recommendation — add_ a weekly one-item SRBAI check-in (1–7). Treat any graduation threshold as a design choice.

**Repetition drives automaticity.** _Claim:_ repeated enactment in a stable context, not the clock, builds the cue–response link. _Evidence:_ Keller 2021 (RCT) found repetition was the main predictor [verified]. Wood & Rünger 2016, DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417 [unverified, not fetched], is described secondarily as defining habit through well-learned cue–behaviour associations ([PMC4566897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4566897)).

## If-then plans and self-monitoring are the best-supported core

**Implementation intentions ("After X, I will Y").** _Claim:_ if-then plans raise goal attainment. _Evidence:_ the main meta-analyses are as follows.

| Study                       | Scope                                              | Effect                                                                               | DOI and status                                                                               | Source                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006   | 94 tests, all goal types                           | **d = 0.65** on goal attainment                                                      | 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1; DOI [unverified], effect confirmed only via secondary sources | [secondary summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-implementation-intentions-when-where-how-goal-achievement/)                                            |
| Bélanger-Gravel et al. 2013 | 26 studies, physical activity                      | **g = 0.31 after the intervention, g = 0.24 at follow-up**; larger with coping plans | 10.1080/17437199.2011.560095 [unverified DOI]                                                | [McMaster summary](https://www.mcmasteroptimalaging.org/full-article/he/meta-analytic-review-effect-implementation-intentions-physical-activity-25322) |
| Silva et al. 2018           | RCTs, physical activity, reinforced plans subgroup | 0.25 (95% CI 0.05–0.45)                                                              | —                                                                                            | [PMC6235272](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6235272/)                                                                                    |

_Replication:_ the effect is robust across many studies, but the 2006 estimate predates pre-registration and includes many small lab studies. Domain estimates are smaller.

**Routine anchors and time anchors.** Routine anchors ("habit stacking") performed **no better than time anchors** in Keller 2021; both raised automaticity [verified]. The "habit stacking" formula and a claimed "2025 Journal of Applied Psychology" study reporting a 64% gain are a popular claim, and the study is likely fabricated ([findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/courses/habit-building/lesson-3-core-skills)). Stawarz et al. 2015 is a 4-week field study (CHI), DOI 10.1145/2702123.2702230 [DOI unverified]. It found that **event-based cues increased automaticity** ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/130626/)).

_Recommendation — use:_ show each stage as a stored sentence: "After [anchor], I will [tiny action] at [place]". Allow a time anchor as an equal alternative. Add an anchor picker (after coffee, after brushing teeth, when I get home) with the check "Does this happen every day?". Expect g ≈ 0.2–0.3 for exercise-type journeys.

**Number of goals and plans.** _Claim:_ planning helps one goal but can backfire with many. _Evidence:_ Dalton & Spiller 2012 (single paper, lab and field), DOI 10.1086/664500 [verified]. Planning helped people with one goal but not people with six, because it made the difficulty salient. Framing the load as manageable restored the benefit ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-664500.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/05/120516152448.htm)). Verhoeven et al. 2013 (two small experiments), DOI 10.1002/ejsp.1963 [verified], found that **one if-then plan reduced snacking but three plans for the same goal did not** ([Utrecht](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/less-is-more-the-effect-of-multiple-implementation-intentions-tar/); [BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/less-more-when-it-comes-beating-bad-habits)). _Replication:_ no independent replication was found for either paper. _Recommendation — use:_ QuestArc's "one active journey" rule, with exactly one active plan per journey. Frame it as "one small step per day".

**Coping plans and the fallback stage.** _Claim:_ barrier management strengthens implementation intentions. _Evidence:_ it was a moderator in Bélanger-Gravel 2013 (MA) [unverified DOI]. _Recommendation — add_ one optional coping plan per journey: "If [obstacle], then I will [fallback]". By default the fallback is the **previous, smaller stage**.

**Mental contrasting / WOOP.** _Claim:_ contrasting a wish with its obstacle improves goal pursuit. _Evidence:_ Cross & Sheffield 2019 (MA, k = 7), DOI 10.1080/17437199.2019.1594332, found **g = 0.28** for health behaviour. Adding implementation intentions did not add to the effect ([Taylor & Francis](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17437199.2019.1594332); [Derby](https://repository.derby.ac.uk/item/93z0q/mental-contrasting-for-health-behaviour-change-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-effects-and-moderator-variables)). Wang, Wang & Gai 2021 (MA, 21 studies, N = 15,907), DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202 [verified], found **g = 0.336**, with signs of publication bias ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)). _Replication:_ the evidence base is thin and possibly inflated. _Recommendation — add_ an optional one-minute WOOP at journey creation, whose "Plan" step fills the coping-plan field. Never make it mandatory or repeat it daily.

**Self-monitoring with feedback.** _Claim:_ recording progress improves attainment, most when it is combined with goals and feedback. _Evidence:_ Harkin et al. 2016 (MA, 138 studies, N = 19,951), DOI 10.1037/bul0000025 [verified], found that monitoring frequency mediated goal attainment. Effects were larger when progress was physically recorded or made public ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/); [working paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/1/bul%20harkin%20raw%20final.pdf)). Its goal-attainment effect of d ≈ 0.40 is [unverified]. Michie et al. 2009 (meta-regression, 122 evaluations), DOI 10.1037/a0016136 [DOI unverified, results verified], found an overall effect of 0.31. Combining **self-monitoring with goal setting, feedback or goal review gave 0.42, against 0.26** without ([PubMed 19916637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916637/); [NCBI CRD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK77075/)). _Recommendation — use:_ the one-tap completion of the bound recurring task is the record. **Add** a weekly review that shows stage progress against the target.

**Start tiny, then grade up.** _Claim:_ tiny starting behaviours grow into larger habits (Fogg's B = MAP and Tiny Habits). _Evidence:_ this is a popular claim. Fogg's own evidence is coaching experience ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/02/14/stanford-scientist-on-proven-habits-that-will-make-you-more-productive.html); [Quartz](https://qz.com/877795/how-to-create-new-good-habits-according-to-stanford-psychologist-b-j-fogg)). Fogg 2009, DOI 10.1145/1541948.1541999 [unverified], is a conceptual paper. A company-reported RCT has no confirmed peer review ([BetterYou](https://www.betteryou.ai/?p=248512), [unverified]). Indirect support is stronger: simpler behaviours automate faster (Lally; Buyalskaya). In addition, **graded tasks** (BCT 8.7) were associated with long-term physical-activity effects in Howlett et al. 2019 (MA), DOI 10.1093/tbm/iby010 [verified]: change d = 0.32, maintenance d = 0.21 ([UHRA](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/handle/2299/20185)). Graded tasks were also a significant moderator in digital interventions in Carraça et al. 2021 (MA), DOI 10.1111/obr.13258 [verified] ([PMC8365685](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8365685/)). _Replication:_ no peer-reviewed RCT of Tiny Habits exists, and no study compares starting tiny with starting at the target dose. _Recommendation — use_ tiny first stages (under two minutes). Keep the **same anchor across stages** so the cue–behaviour link carries over. Never call Tiny Habits "scientifically proven" in copy.

**Identity framing ("I am a walker").** _Claim:_ noun-based identity wording changes behaviour. _Evidence:_ Bryan et al. 2011, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1103343108, reported an 11–14 point gain in turnout ([FORRT](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1073/pnas.1103343108/)). _Replication:_ **failed.** Gerber et al. 2016 found no difference ([PMC5087036](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5087036)). A pre-registered "psychologically authentic" replication in 2020 found that behaviour is unaffected ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/abs/voting-behavior-is-unaffected-by-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence-from-a-psychologically-authentic-replication/7CA9BE69C3213D5030A2BD11A5C53450); [J-PAL](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/voting-behavior-unaffected-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence); critique blog [Replication Index](https://replicationindex.com/2019/12/02/christopher-j-bryan-claims-replicators-p-hack-to-get-non-significant-results-i-claim-he-p-hacked-his-original-results/)). _Recommendation — avoid_ it as a mechanism. It is acceptable as graduation flavour text.

## Lapse handling decides whether a miss becomes a quit

**Tolerant streaks.** _Claim:_ a broken streak lowers later engagement, more so when people blame themselves and less when the streak can be repaired. _Evidence:_ Silverman & Barasch 2023 (7 studies, lab and field), DOI [10.1093/jcr/ucac029](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029) [verified] ([UDel manuscript](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb); [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). In language-app field data, users whose streak broke switched to another language rather than restarting ([CU Boulder news](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). _Replication:_ none was found, and some of the studies use hypothetical scenarios. Grey literature exists: a claim that Duolingo's streak freeze cut churn by 21% has no primary source ([case study](https://jiwonvyoo.github.io/portfolio/case/duolingo/duo.html)), and Busuu reported +15% sessions ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.Com/blog/duolingo-streak-feature-app-engagement-growth)). A 2026 case study finds that streaks drive retention but **do not build app-independent habits** ([IJMAT](https://www.wr-publishing.org/index.php/ijmat/article/view/908)). The underlying engagement claim rests on a qualitative study: Renfree et al. 2016 found that reminders and streaks create dependency and "fragile" habits that collapse when users leave the app ([CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/79500911.pdf); [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/dont-kick-the-habit-the-role-of-dependency-in-habit-formation-app/)). _Recommendation — use_ the tolerant streak, with these details. A single miss shows as a neutral hollow dot, never a red break. When a run does end, show the total and best runs, never "reset to 0". The break screen uses no-blame copy. **Avoid** purchasable freezes.

**What-the-hell and abstinence-violation effects.** _Claim:_ perceiving that a rigid rule has been broken triggers giving up. _Evidence:_ classic small lab studies, all [unverified DOIs]. They include Herman & Mack 1975 (10.1111/j.1467-6494.1975.tb00727.x), Polivy & Herman 1985 (10.1037/0003-066X.40.2.193) and Polivy, Herman & Deo 2010 (10.1016/j.appet.2010.07.015), in which merely _believing_ a slice was large was enough ([PsyBlog](https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=13881); [overview](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Counterregulatory%20eating)). Curry, Marlatt & Gordon 1987 (10.1037/0022-006X.55.2.145) is a correlational smoking study. Some people respond to a lapse with course correction instead ([Phys.org on Zemack-Rugar 2014](https://phys.org/news/2014-05-consumer-behavior-response-discipline-key.pdf)). _Replication:_ no modern pre-registered replication exists, and a critique points to Tomiyama et al. 2009 [unverified]. _Recommendation — use_ "never miss twice" and self-recovering HP as countermeasures. **Avoid** "perfect week" badges, "0/7" displays and trait language such as "you're inconsistent".

**Self-compassion.** _Claim:_ kindness after failure increases the motivation to try again. _Evidence:_

| Wohl et al. 2010               | Prospective, correlational      | Self-forgiveness predicted less procrastination later                                      | [10.1016/j.paid.2010.01.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.01.029) [verified] | [BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/cure-procrastination-forgive-yourself) |
| Adams & Leary 2007             | Lab study                       | Self-compassion after a preload reduced later eating among restrictive eaters              | [verified]                                                                          | [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/782823)                   |

An "Adriaanse 2014" paper on this topic could not be found [unverified]; do not cite it. _Replication:_ the evidence comes from small samples, and no large replications were found. _Recommendation — use_ self-compassionate copy paired with a concrete next step, for example "Missing a day is normal. What's one small step today?". Render low HP as "resting", never "dying".

**Emergency reserves.** _Claim:_ a challenging goal with a limited, slightly costly reserve of skips beats both easy and rigid goals. _Evidence:_ Sharif & Shu 2017 (multi-study, lab and field), DOI [10.1509/jmr.15.0231](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.15.0231) [verified] ([UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/); [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Designing-More-Effective-Goals-by-Using-Emergency-Reserves-A-Field-Experiment.pdf)). Sharif & Shu 2021 (OBHDP 163) [verified] found that reserves aid persistence after a subgoal fails ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:163:y:2021:i:c:p:17-29); [author PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf)). _Replication:_ both papers come from the same authors. _Recommendation:_ "never miss twice" already acts as a bounded reserve, so **keep it bounded**. Do not add unlimited free skips. A pause or sick mode is a different thing and should stay available.

**Comeback acknowledgement.** _Claim:_ rewarding a return after a miss is the most effective single mechanic tested for exercise. _Evidence:_ Milkman et al. 2021 (Mega, 54 four-week arms, ~61,293 gym members), DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4 [verified via Caltech record and press; DOI marked unverified in one note]. **45% of arms raised weekly visits by 9–27%.** Expert forecasters could not predict which arms would work, and **only ~8% had effects that lasted beyond four weeks** ([Caltech](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/mys88-cc756); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost); [Mathematica](https://mathematica.org/publications/megastudies-improve-the-impact-of-applied-behavioural-science); [Berkeley CDSS](https://cdss.berkeley.edu/events/mega-studies-improve-impact-applied-behavioral-science)). The top arm gave a bonus of about $0.09 for returning after a missed planned workout. Penn Today reports **+27%** ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits)), while CNN reports about **16% against the planning-plus-reminders baseline** ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/health/cnn-health/2021/12/08/these-are-the-best-ways-to-up-your-workout-habits-according-to-study-of-over-60000-people/)). The two figures likely use different comparison groups; this cannot be resolved without nature.com. A blog analysis puts the absolute gain at 0.14–0.40 extra visits per week on a base of 1.48 [unverified], and questions multiple comparisons ([jcx.au](https://blog.jcx.au/posts/megastudy-scepticism)). _Replication:_ one megastudy, not yet replicated. _Recommendation — add_ a deterministic comeback moment on the completion after a miss. In calm mode it is a warm line: "Comeback — arc kept." In RPG mode it is a fixed bonus guardian hit or story beat. Misses are defined against the user's **own planned schedule** from the recurring task, as in the winning arm.

**Fresh starts.** _Claim:_ temporal landmarks prompt aspirational behaviour. _Evidence:_ Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 (archival), DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 [verified] ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901); [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)). Beshears et al. 2021 (Field exp., N = 6,082), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2021.06.005 [verified], found that fresh-start framing raised retirement savings, while placebo dates and New Year framing did not ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:167:y:2021:i:c:p:72-87); [UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/fresh-start-framing-boosts-retirement-plan-participation/); [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news-info/daily-news/how-fresh-start-framing-can-boost-retirement-savings)). A 2026 dissertation (not peer-reviewed) reports that the effect appears only for **self-chosen** start dates ([eGrove](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/etd/2026)). Koo, Dai et al. (in press, status unconfirmed) report that an upcoming landmark licenses slacking on goals already under way ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson-review.ucla.edu/fresh-start)). _Replication:_ no multi-lab replication. _Recommendation — add_ self-chosen start dates (Monday, the 1st, a birthday) at journey creation, and landmark restarts after a run has ended. **Avoid** "start fresh next week" prompts to users who are actively progressing.

## The game layer works modestly, so keep it deterministic and optional

**Autonomy first (self-determination theory).** _Claim:_ need-supportive, autonomous motivation predicts health behaviour, and interventions built on it work. _Evidence:_ Ng et al. 2012 (MA, 184 data sets, mostly correlational), DOI 10.1177/1745691612447309. The data-set count is [verified] and the pooled correlations are [unverified] ([Ewha](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/self-determination-theory-applied-to-health-contexts-a-meta-analy/)). Ntoumanis et al. 2021 (MA, 73 studies), DOI 10.1080/17437199.2020.1718529 [verified abstract], found small positive effects ([Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-self-determination-theory-informed-interventio/); [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/155741/)). Sheeran et al. 2020 (MA of RCTs), DOI 10.1037/ccp0000485 [DOI unverified], found **d+ = 0.23, still significant after bias correction** ([White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/164092/3/SDT%20ABM%202020.pdf)). _Replication:_ this is the most robust motivational base in the review, though effects are small and measures self-reported. _Recommendation — use:_ keep calm mode as the default, RPG as an explicit opt-in, user-chosen journeys and targets, and penalty-free pause. Calm mode must be a full long-term state, not a downgrade.

**Gamification overall.** _Claim:_ gamification improves engagement and behaviour. _Evidence:_

_Replication:_ effects are heterogeneous, fade with novelty, and no study isolates _opt-in_ gamification. _Recommendation — use_ the RPG layer as optional, low-stakes feedback and expect 0.2–0.4 SD while it is active. Late journey stages should need less game scaffolding.

**XP, levels and the undermining effect.** _Claim:_ expected, tangible, contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, while informational feedback and praise do not. _Evidence:_ Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (MA, 128 experiments), DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627 [verified for contingency effects]: engagement-contingent rewards d = −0.40, completion-contingent −0.36, performance-contingent −0.28 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2731358/); [UW PDF](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)). For tangible rewards d ≈ −0.34, and verbal praise d ≈ +0.33; unexpected rewards did not undermine. These three figures are [unverified]. Cameron & Pierce 1994 (DOI 10.3102/00346543064003363 [unverified]) disputed the conclusion. Cameron, Banko & Pierce 2001 conceded harm for high-interest tasks with expected, tangible rewards loosely tied to performance ([Ewha](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/extrinsic-rewards-and-intrinsic-motivation-in-education-reconside/); [Deci et al. 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001); [SDT PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)). _Replication:_ the debate is long-standing, and **no experiment tests whether virtual XP undermines motivation like tangible rewards**. _Recommendation — use_ fixed XP per completion and cosmetic-only levels, framed informationally ("20 walks done"). **Avoid** announcing XP before tasks the user already enjoys, and avoid XP for time-on-task. Turning RPG off must never feel like losing everything.

**Guardians, badges and leaderboards.** _Claim:_ social structure carries the behavioural effect, and leaderboards demotivate low performers. _Evidence:_ Patel et al. 2019, STEP UP (RCT, n = 602), DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.3505 [DOI unverified, results verified]. Compared with control, **competition added 920 steps/day, support 689 and collaboration 637**. **Only competition persisted after the game ended (+569).** The trial was funded by Deloitte and the Penn Nudge Unit ([PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/); [Penn Medicine](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/901675)). The notes cite the issue as both 179(11) and 179(12). BE FIT 2017 (DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.3458) is [unverified]. Leaderboard studies are mixed: low ranks demotivate some users, while other studies report more effort ([Emerald 2023](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-12-2021-0897/full/html); [ScholarSpace](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/70776); [arXiv 1707.03704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03704v1); [PMC4698889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4698889); [Jia 2018](https://scholarworks.iupui.edu/bitstream/1805/17645/1/Jia_2018_designing.pdf); [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000169056/152385336); [Warwick](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/173148/)). "Social incentive" was a significant moderator in digital interventions (Carraça 2021). _Recommendation:_ **keep no leaderboards**, and document it internally as a wellbeing and values choice: competition was the strongest arm. **Use** guardians as collaborative opponents worn down by completions, never punishing misses. **Use** badges only as stage milestones. **Add** an opt-in supporter or party with a weekly digest (the support arm), and apply "never miss twice" to the party so that one member cannot "break" it.

**Self-recovering HP and loss framing.** _Claim:_ loss framing boosts behaviour during an intervention but not afterwards, and punishment mechanics backfire. _Evidence:_ Patel et al. 2016 (RCT, n = 281), DOI [10.7326/M15-1635](https://doi.org/10.7326/M15-1635) [verified]. Only the loss arm beat control (45% vs 30% of days at goal), and **nothing persisted** after incentives ended ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/2016-03-24/research/prospect-losing-money-pushes-people-achieve-fitness-goals); [NCT02030119](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02030119)). Diefenbach & Müssig 2019 (mixed methods, field study n = 45), DOI [10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004) [verified], found that **all Habitica participants experienced counterproductive effects**, such as being punished during productive periods ([LMU](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77668)). _Recommendation — use_ HP only as informational (last seven days), self-healing and optional. **Add** a backfill window ("done yesterday") and a pause/sick mode. **Avoid** item loss, death or resets.

**Goal gradient and endowed progress.** _Claim:_ effort accelerates near a goal, and a head start speeds completion. _Evidence:_ Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, DOI 10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39 [verified], found ~20% shorter gaps between purchases near the reward ([Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf); [Columbia summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration); discrepant blog figures at [Alfred](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/goal-gradient-effect)). Post-reward drops are [unverified]. Nunes & Drèze 2006, DOI 10.1086/500480 [unverified], found ~19% vs ~34% completion. The progress principle (Amabile & Kramer 2011) is a correlational diary study [unverified] ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins)). _Replication:_ none independent. _Recommendation — use_ short stages and show the distance to the _next_ stage. When a stage or guardian falls, immediately show the next stage's first step. Endowed progress is acceptable only if it is honest ("setup counts as step one").

**Narrative.** _Claim:_ story improves adherence. _Evidence:_ game fiction was a moderator in Sailer & Homner, based on few studies. Schwarz et al. 2021 (cluster RCT), DOI 10.3390/ijerph18147444 [verified], found low engagement ([PMC8305797](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8305797/)). In a 2025 medication-app pilot, storytelling "was not favored" ([PMC11781755](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11781755/)). Transportation theory and its meta-analysis are [unverified] (Green & Brock 2000, 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701; van Laer 2014, 10.1086/673383). The claim that Zombies, Run! improves adherence is a popular claim. _Recommendation — use lightly:_ short, optional, unexpected story beats, never a gate before logging.

**Temptation bundling.** _Claim:_ pairing a treat with the habit raises it. _Evidence:_ Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2013.1784 [verified], found **+51%** gym visits at first, decaying over time ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25843979/); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1784)). The larger Kirgios et al. 2020 (N = 6,792), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2020.09.003 [DOI unverified], found **+10–12%** weekly workouts. Teaching bundling added little beyond giving the audiobook ([Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/TemptationBundling.pdf); [commentary](https://beoutlines.blogspot.com/2023/08/kirgios-mandel-park-et-al-2020-on.html); [Rotman](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/rotman-management-magazine/20210501/282973927858878)). _Replication:_ replicated at scale with a much smaller effect. _Recommendation — add_ an optional "pair it with" field per stage, shown in the plan sentence. Never lock the treat behind an in-app reward.

**Commitment devices.** Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010 (review), DOI 10.1146/annurev.economics.102308.124324 [unverified], finds low take-up and forfeited stakes. _Recommendation — avoid_ money contracts. **Add** at most a soft, self-written one-line promise.

**Random rewards, login bonuses and dark patterns.** _Claim:_ variable-ratio rewards drive compulsive use. _Evidence:_ the lab basis is Ferster & Skinner 1957 [unverified]. **No RCT shows that random rewards improve health outcomes.** Loot boxes correlate with problem gambling (Zendle & Cairns 2018, 10.1371/journal.pone.0206767 [unverified]). The relevant ethics and law are as follows, all [unverified] unless marked:

- Berdichevsky & Neuenschwander 1999, 10.1145/301353.301410: disclose persuasion and use none the user would not consent to.
- Fogg 2003 on the ethics of persuasive technology.
- Gray et al. 2018 on dark patterns, 10.1145/3173574.3174108.
- Mathur et al. 2019 on dark patterns at scale, 10.1145/3359183.
- EU DSA Art. 25 bans manipulative interfaces on online platforms ([verified], [text](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/de-de/out-law/analyse/dark-patterns-digital-regulation-consumer-law)). A standalone habit app is likely not an "online platform", but the UCPD applies.
- The planned Digital Fairness Act targets "penalties for disengagement". Its proposal is expected around Q4 2026 and was **not confirmed** as of 5 Nov 2026 ([EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act); [Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102m222/digital-fairness-act-european-commission-publishes-responses-to-consultation); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/08/01/eu-consults-on-digital-fairness-act-big-changes-ahead-for-consumer-facing-platforms/)).

_Recommendation — keep_ no random rewards and no login bonuses. **Avoid** confirmshaming, streak-loss threat notifications and obstructed deletion. **Add** a "How QuestArc motivates you" page.

**Reminders.** _Claim:_ reminders support repetition but hinder automaticity. _Evidence:_ Stawarz 2015 (small field study; sample size [unverified]) [verified finding]. A 2016 medication follow-up abstract found that reminder users forgot less often but reported low automaticity ([Frontiers abstract](https://www.frontiersin.org/10.3389/conf.FPUBH.2016.01.00066/event_abstract); later paper [UCL PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1522125/7/Stawarz_2055207616678707.pdf)). _Replication:_ small samples, and no RCT of fading schedules exists. _Recommendation — use_ reminders that restate the plan ("After lunch → walk 5 min"), timed to the anchor. **Fade** them as completion and automaticity rise.

**Success metric.** Yardley et al. 2016 (10.1016/j.amepre.2016.06.015 [unverified]) separates "effective engagement" with the behaviour from use of the app. Eysenbach 2005 (10.2196/jmir.7.1.e11 [unverified]) describes the "law of attrition". Laranjo et al. 2021 (MA, 28 RCTs) found that apps and trackers added ~1,850 steps/day, with high heterogeneity ([BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/2021/01/26/do-smartphone-applications-and-activity-trackers-increase-physical-activity-in-adults/); [NOVA PDF](https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/147424/1/422.full.pdf); DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102892 [unverified]). _Recommendation — measure_ completion over 8–12 weeks plus SRBAI. Do not measure daily active users or streak length.

## Quotes do nothing measurable; the user's own "why" and actionable nuggets do

**Generic motivational quotes.** _Claim:_ inspirational quotes motivate behaviour. _Evidence:_ **this is a popular claim, and no experiment was found.** Trait inspiration and inspiration about one's own goal predict goal progress correlationally (Milyavskaya et al. 2012, 10.1016/j.paid.2011.08.031; sample sizes and effects not retrieved, [reference list](https://typeset.io/pdf/nostalgia-evoked-inspiration-mediating-mechanisms-and-cenra9swi8.pdf)). Thrash & Elliot 2003 (10.1037/0022-3514.84.4.871 [unverified]) describes inspiration as something that _happens to_ a person and cannot be produced on demand. Web claims are untraceable ([example](https://ischoolconnect.com/blog/?p=16279)). The feared harm also fails: Wood et al. 2009 (10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02370.x, seen only on a [secondary page](https://studiessuggest.pages.dev/stories/positive-affirmations-backfire-low-self-esteem.html)) reported that positive self-statements backfire for people with low self-esteem. **The replication failed:** Flynn & Bordieri 2020 found no effects on mood, self-esteem or goal completion ([ACBS](https://contextualscience.org/publications/flynn_bordieri_2020); [abstract](https://contextualscience.org/node/29886)). The real risks of quotes are irrelevance, wear-out and preachiness. _Recommendation — avoid_ a daily quote feature. If quotes stay for RPG flavour, they should be off by default, appear only at milestones, be non-directive, and never be pushed.

**The user's own "why".** _Claim:_ reflecting on one's own values improves health behaviour. _Evidence:_ Epton et al. 2015 (MA, 144 tests), DOI 10.1037/hea0000116 [verified]: acceptance d = 0.17, intentions d = 0.14, **behaviour d = 0.32** ([PubMed 25133846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25133846/); [Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/the-impact-of-self-affirmation-on-health-behavior-change-a-meta-a/)). A second 2015 meta-analysis found behaviour d+ ≈ 0.27; its authorship is unconfirmed, likely Sweeney & Moyer ([Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/self-affirmation-and-responses-to-health-messages-a-meta-analysis/)). Values writing did not change goal completion in Flynn & Bordieri Study 2. _Caveat:_ self-affirmation mainly reduces defensiveness to threatening messages, so the transfer to "own reasons in own words" is supported by SDT rather than shown directly. _Recommendation — use:_ ask once per journey (optionally) "Why does this matter to you?". Show it back at stations and after a miss.

**Learning nuggets.** _Claim:_ brief habit-science advice changes behaviour. _Evidence:_ the Ten Top Tips RCT is Beeken et al. 2017 (n = 537). It found **0.87 kg more weight lost at 3 months**; by 24 months the control group had caught up; cost ~£23 per person ([UCL PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1530679/1/ijo2016206a.pdf); [ISRCTN16347068](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN16347068)). The intervention was a package of _actionable_ tips plus self-monitoring, built on Gardner, Lally & Wardle 2012 (10.3399/bjgp12X659466 [unverified]). For microlearning, a meta-analysis in higher education ([JMTR PDF](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/08/28/jmtr_24_22.pdf)) and De Gagne 2019 ([secondary](https://www.visualsp.com/blog/8-studies-that-prove-microlearning-cant-be-ignored/)) show knowledge gains, not behaviour change. Vendor figures are unsourced ([Arist](https://www.arist.co/post/2025-meta-analysis-microlearning-improves-job-behavior-50-percent/blogrelixir)). Spacing is robust (Dunlosky 2013 [unverified]). Messaging meta-analyses also bear on delivery:

_Recommendation — add_ one short, actionable "try this" nugget per station, matched to the stage. A suggested sequence: choose a reliable cue → shrink the action → plan for a missed day → one miss is fine → pair it with something you like → you will need the app less. Re-show the "one miss is fine" nugget after a miss (spaced, at the moment of need). Never use trivia without an action, and never repeat nuggets otherwise.

**Timing, tone and fatigue.** _Timing:_ HeartSteps (micro-randomized trial; Klasnja 2019) found that contextual suggestions raised steps by **167 (66%) in the next 30 minutes, an effect that faded over six weeks**; generic anti-sedentary messages did nothing ([PMC6401341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341); [PubMed 30192907](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30192907/); reanalyses [arXiv 2502.13500](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13500), [arXiv 2410.15049](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15049)). Just-in-time adaptive intervention design treats "deliver nothing" as a valid option and delivers only when the user is receptive (Nahum-Shani 2018, 10.1007/s12160-016-9830-8, [PMC5364076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364076); [Künzler](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/dfk/research/kunzler-receptivity/kunzler-receptivity.pdf)). _Tone:_ controlling language provokes reactance (Rains 2013 MA, 10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x, [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093682); Miller et al. 2007, [via PMC6393822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6393822/); Stok & Renner 2017, [EHPS](https://ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/view/2586)). One trial found no effect of autonomy-supportive wording (Altendorf 2019, [Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/should-or-could-testing-the-use-of-autonomy-supportive-language-a/)). A 2026 meta-analysis reports the reactance cost is small but accumulates ([PsyPost, unverified](https://www.psypost.org/forceful-language-makes-people-resist-health-advice/)). Dillard & Shen 2005 is [unverified]. _Fatigue:_ repetition causes avoidance through overexposure, redundancy, exhaustion and boredom (So, Kim & Cohen 2017, [Comm. Monographs](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/03637751.2016.1250429); [PMC5772987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5772987); [review](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/no-more-messages-a-systematic-review-of-predictors-and-outcomes-o/)). Stawarz 2015 found **positive reinforcement had no effect on automaticity**, so celebratory messages are for enjoyment, not habit building. _How:_ show messages in the app at receptive moments: station reached, stage choice, after a miss. That fits both hosts, since the Super Productivity plugin and the Linux phone app can surface them where the task is completed, without push. Use wording such as "you could" and "many people find", make every message dismissible, and use fewer as stages progress.

## What changes in QuestArc's design rules

The evidence turns into ten concrete rule changes:

1. **Graduation is gated on automaticity, not days.** A habit graduates when the one-item SRBAI stays high (for example ≥ 5–6 of 7 for two to three consecutive weekly check-ins), completion is consistent, and reminders have already faded without a drop. The threshold is a design choice. Drop any "66 days" trigger, including the one suggested in one note. Add check-ins at two and six weeks after graduation and a one-tap "bring it back". Keep graduated habits visible as achievements.
2. **A comeback acknowledgement after a miss.** The first completion after a miss gets a fixed, non-random acknowledgement: a line in calm mode, a bonus hit in RPG mode.
3. **A temptation-bundling field.** Each stage gets an optional "pair it with" field, shown in the plan sentence.
4. **A weekly automaticity check-in** ("I do this automatically", 1–7). It feeds graduation, stage advancement and reminder fading.
5. **The fallback plan is the previous, smaller stage.** It is the default coping plan; stepping back carries no penalty, and the second consecutive miss offers it.
6. **Self-chosen start dates** (today, Monday, the 1st, a chosen date) at journey creation, and landmark restarts only after a run has ended.
7. **The user's own "why" replaces quotes.** It is captured once and shown at stations and after misses. Quotes are off by default, if they exist at all.
8. **Reminders restate the plan and fade:** daily, then weekly, then off as automaticity rises, with an explicit message such as "you won't need me for this one".
9. **Plus smaller rules:**
   - the plan is stored as anchor, action and place, and either a routine or a time anchor is allowed;
   - misses are defined against the recurring task's own schedule;
   - a backfill window ("done yesterday") and a pause/sick mode;
   - a neutral gap display, plus total and best runs instead of a reset;
   - no-blame, autonomy-supportive copy throughout;
   - optional WOOP and supporter;
   - nuggets in the app at receptive moments only;
   - a "How QuestArc motivates you" disclosure page.
10. **The success metric is 8–12-week completion plus SRBAI**, never app opens.

## Open evidence gaps

The largest gap is QuestArc's signature: **no study measures behaviour after tracking and reminders are deliberately removed once automaticity is high**. Second, no study tests multi-stage journeys in which the behaviour grows. It is unknown whether the automaticity of "walk 5 minutes" transfers to "walk 15 minutes". No validated SRBAI cut-off for "formed" exists, and the 2024 single-item paper's details are unconfirmed. No study compares tolerating zero, one or two consecutive misses. No study tests whether virtual XP undermines intrinsic motivation, compares opt-in with mandatory gamification, or isolates narrative in a habit app. No RCT tests reminder-fading schedules, self-compassion copy inside an app, or quotes and nuggets against behaviour.

Several studies the design leans on are unreplicated single papers: Dalton & Spiller, Verhoeven, goal gradient, endowed progress, Silverman & Barasch, Sharif & Shu and the Milkman top arm. Specific figures need checking against primary texts:

- the Gollwitzer & Sheeran d = 0.65, the Harkin d = 0.40 and the Deci tangible-reward and praise values;
- Mazeas' final published figures and follow-up result;
- the Milkman arm effects (27% against 16%) and its baseline, which needs nature.com;
- the Keller 23% figure and the STEP UP issue number.

The status of the Digital Fairness Act and the DSA Article 25 guidelines must be re-checked at publication. QuestArc can fill some of these gaps itself, ethically, with opt-in A/B tests of story beats, comeback acknowledgements and reminder fading, measured by 8–12-week completion and SRBAI.

## Conclusion

QuestArc's design is closer to the evidence than most habit apps. Its strongest choices are calm defaults, a single journey, tolerant lapses and no random rewards. These are not the "fun" parts; they are the parts that keep a miss from turning into a quit and keep the app from becoming the cue. The literature points to a reframing: the RPG layer is a temporary scaffold whose job is to hand off to context-cued automaticity, and the app's measure of success is how little the user needs it by the last stage. That makes graduation on measured automaticity the feature most worth building well, and also the one QuestArc is best placed to generate evidence for.

Two choices are value judgements rather than evidence-maximising ones, and the team should own them as such. Excluding competition gives up the one social mechanic whose effect persisted in STEP UP. Avoiding quotes in favour of the user's own words trades familiar app "feel" for the only motivational-message approach with meta-analytic support.
