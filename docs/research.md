# Repetition, not rewards, builds QuestArc's habits

The science supports QuestArc's calm core far more than its game layer. Habits form through **repeated performance of one small action in a stable context**. Automaticity rises along a curve that took a **median of 59–66 days in trials but ranged from 4 to 335 days** across individuals ([Singh et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/)). **A single missed day did not materially hurt habit formation**, while very inconsistent performance did ([BPS on Lally 2010](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)). The best-evidenced levers QuestArc already uses or can add cheaply are these: if-then plans tied to an existing routine, self-monitoring with feedback and goal review, autonomy-supportive design, tolerance of single lapses, a comeback acknowledgement after a miss, and graduation once the habit runs without the app. The game elements (XP, levels, guardians, narrative) have **small, heterogeneous and fading effects** (behavioural g ≈ 0.25 in learning contexts; g ≈ 0.42 for physical activity, both fragile). They are worth keeping as an optional, deterministic, non-punitive layer, but QuestArc should not present them as the mechanism of change. Reminders help repetition but **can hinder automaticity** because the phone becomes the cue, so they should restate the user's own plan and fade. Generic motivational quotes have **no experimental evidence of changing behaviour**. Short, actionable learning nuggets have modest support from a habit-advice RCT and from message-intervention meta-analyses. A better replacement for quotes is the user's own "why", backed by a self-affirmation meta-analysis (behaviour d = 0.32). Several effects the app could lean on rest on single, unreplicated papers: goal gradient, endowed progress, implementation-intention overload, the what-the-hell effect and self-compassion. The biggest gap is QuestArc's own signature feature: no study tests what happens when tracking is removed after a habit becomes automatic.

**How to read this report.** Each technique is given as _claim → evidence strength → replication status → recommendation (use / add / avoid) and how_. Strength labels are MA (meta-analysis or systematic review), Mega (megastudy), RCT, Field exp., Single study, Diary/Correlational, and Popular claim. Every citation keeps the verification marking from the research notes. **[verified]** means a researcher confirmed the source or figure in the research session from a publisher, PubMed, repository or abstract page. **[unverified]** means a DOI, figure or detail came from prior knowledge or a secondary source and was not checked against the primary text. The Milkman et al. 2021 gym megastudy was first known only from secondary sources; its figures were then **checked against the paper's full text** in PubMed Central ([PMC8822539](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)). ## Habits form in two to five months, and one miss does not break them

**Time to automaticity.** _Claim:_ the "21 days" rule is false, and formation time varies widely. _Evidence:_ Lally et al. 2010 is a single observational study (n = 96 recruited, 82 completed, 84 days), DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674 [verified]. In it, the time to 95% of the automaticity plateau had a median of 66 days (range 18–254). That median comes only from the roughly 39 participants whose data fitted the curve, and the upper range is extrapolated beyond the observation window ([The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit); [UCL 2009](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). Keller et al. 2021 is an RCT (n = 192), DOI 10.1111/bjhp.12504 [verified]. It found a **median of 59 days to peak automaticity** among people who formed habits ([PubMed 33405284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33405284/); [Refubium](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/handle/fub188/31524)). Only **23% reached the study's predefined habit threshold**; that figure comes from a secondary source and is [unverified: not in the abstract; Europe PMC lists no open full text, Refubium answers automated requests with a bot check and was not used, and the Minerva Access (Melbourne) copy is refused by this environment's proxy] ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-habit-systematic-review-59-66-335-days/)). Singh et al. 2024 is an MA, DOI 10.3390/healthcare12232488 [verified]. It pooled the four studies that reported formation time: medians 59–66 days, means 106–154 days, individual range 4–335 days. It also found that morning and self-selected habits were stronger ([PubMed 39685110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250124/Healthy-habits-take-longer-to-form-than-expected.aspx)). Buyalskaya et al. 2023 is a large observational study (more than 12 million gym check-ins and more than 40 million handwashing observations), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2216115120 [verified]. It found that **handwashing became predictable in weeks and gym going in months**. The strongest predictor of attendance was **time since the last visit**, and habitual gym-goers responded less to incentives ([PMC10151500](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/04/230417155750.htm)). _Replication:_ the range of findings converges across studies, but no single figure replicates. A multi-centre replication of Lally was proposed as a registered report, and its publication status was not verified ([PCI RR preprint](https://rr.peercommunityin.org/download/t_recommendations.reply_pdf.8382af6b73bdd0f7.6d616e757363726970745f7072657072696e742e706466.pdf)). _Recommendation — use:_ never promise a day count. Copy should say "most simple habits take about two to five months, and it varies a lot". Prefer daily cadence and self-chosen habits for early stages.

**Missing once.** _Claim:_ one missed opportunity does not derail formation, but inconsistency does. _Evidence:_ Lally 2010 (single study) [verified] ([BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit); [UCL blog](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/bsh/2012/06/29); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/70653523.pdf)). Buyalskaya 2023 supports it: risk rises with the length of the gap. _Replication:_ this rests on few missed-day data points and has not been independently replicated. _Recommendation — use_ "never miss twice", but describe it honestly as a heuristic. No study compares tolerating zero, one or two consecutive misses.

**Measuring automaticity.** _Claim:_ automaticity can be measured with the 4-item Self-Report Behavioural Automaticity Index (SRBAI), the automaticity subscale of the Self-Report Habit Index. _Evidence:_ Gardner et al. 2012 showed the SRBAI is reliable and predictive, DOI 10.1186/1479-5868-9-102 [verified] ([PMC3552971](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3552971/)). Its items complete the stem "Behaviour X is something…" with "I do automatically", "I do without having to consciously remember", "I do without thinking" and "I start doing before I realise I'm doing it" [verified, full text]. A 2024 secondary analysis by Gardner, Lally & Rebar of 16 datasets (16,838 participants), DOI 10.1111/aphw.12600 (_Appl Psychol Health Well Being_ 16(4):2438–2457) [verified], found that each of the four items alone met the validity criteria and recommended "Behaviour X is something I do automatically" because it "tended to most closely replicate" the full scale ([PubMed 39324279](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39324279/)). _Replication:_ the SRBAI is widely used, but **no validated cut-off for "habit formed" exists**. _Recommendation — add_ a weekly one-item SRBAI check-in (1–7). Treat any graduation threshold as a design choice.

**Repetition drives automaticity.** _Claim:_ repeated enactment in a stable context, not the clock, builds the cue–response link. _Evidence:_ Keller 2021 (RCT) found repetition was the main predictor [verified]. Wood & Rünger 2016, DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417 (_Annu Rev Psychol_ 67:289–314) [verified, [PubMed 26361052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/)], is described secondarily as defining habit through well-learned cue–behaviour associations ([PMC4566897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4566897)).

## If-then plans and self-monitoring are the best-supported core

**Implementation intentions ("After X, I will Y").** _Claim:_ if-then plans raise goal attainment. _Evidence:_ the main meta-analyses are as follows.

| Study                       | Scope                                              | Effect                                                                               | DOI and status                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Source                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006   | 94 tests, all goal types                           | **d = 0.65** on goal attainment                                                      | 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1 (_Adv Exp Soc Psychol_ 38:69–119) [verified, Crossref; d = .65 across 94 tests in the abstract]                                                                                                                                                                       | [KOPS](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/10973)                                                                                            |
| Bélanger-Gravel et al. 2013 | 26 studies, physical activity                      | **g = 0.31 after the intervention, g = 0.24 at follow-up**; larger with coping plans | 10.1080/17437199.2011.560095 (_Health Psychol Rev_ 7(1):23–54, online 2011) [DOI verified, Crossref; g values unverified: from the secondary summary only; Europe PMC has no record, Crossref has no abstract, and the publisher, PEDro and McMaster pages are refused by this environment's proxy] | [McMaster summary](https://www.mcmasteroptimalaging.org/full-article/he/meta-analytic-review-effect-implementation-intentions-physical-activity-25322) |
| Silva et al. 2018           | RCTs, physical activity, reinforced plans subgroup | 0.25 (95% CI 0.05–0.45)                                                              | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | [PMC6235272](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6235272/)                                                                                    |

_Replication:_ the effect is robust across many studies, but the 2006 estimate predates pre-registration and includes many small lab studies. Domain estimates are smaller. A 2024 update by Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (642 tests, DOI 10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563, _Eur Rev Soc Psychol_ 36(1):162–194) found .27 ≤ d ≤ .66 across outcome types, an overall d = .36 (trim and fill .35), and a **robust Bayesian bias-corrected d = .15** with strong evidence of publication bias. For **behaviour change d = .27 (301 tests), and for physical activity only d = .14 (64 tests)** [verified, full text at [KOPS](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)]. The 0.65 figure therefore overstates what to expect for a habit app: plan for a small effect.

**Routine anchors and time anchors.** Routine anchors ("habit stacking") performed **no better than time anchors** in Keller 2021; both raised automaticity [verified]. The "habit stacking" formula and a claimed "2025 Journal of Applied Psychology" study reporting a 64% gain are a popular claim, and the study is likely fabricated ([findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/courses/habit-building/lesson-3-core-skills)). Stawarz et al. 2015 is a 4-week field study (CHI), DOI 10.1145/2702123.2702230 [verified, Crossref: _Proc. CHI 2015_, pp. 2653–2662]. It found that **event-based cues increased automaticity** ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/130626/)).

_Recommendation — use:_ show each stage as a stored sentence: "After [anchor], I will [tiny action] at [place]". Allow a time anchor as an equal alternative. Add an anchor picker (after coffee, after brushing teeth, when I get home) with the check "Does this happen every day?". Expect g ≈ 0.2–0.3 for exercise-type journeys.

**Number of goals and plans.** _Claim:_ planning helps one goal but can backfire with many. _Evidence:_ Dalton & Spiller 2012 (single paper, lab and field), DOI 10.1086/664500 [verified]. Planning helped people with one goal but not people with six, because it made the difficulty salient. Framing the load as manageable restored the benefit ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-664500.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/05/120516152448.htm)). Verhoeven et al. 2013 (two small experiments), DOI 10.1002/ejsp.1963 [verified], found that **one if-then plan reduced snacking but three plans for the same goal did not** ([Utrecht](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/less-is-more-the-effect-of-multiple-implementation-intentions-tar/); [BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/less-more-when-it-comes-beating-bad-habits)). _Replication:_ no independent replication was found for either paper. _Recommendation — use:_ QuestArc's "one active journey" rule, with exactly one active plan per journey. Frame it as "one small step per day".

**Coping plans and the fallback stage.** _Claim:_ barrier management strengthens implementation intentions. _Evidence:_ it was a moderator in Bélanger-Gravel 2013 (MA) [DOI verified, Crossref; moderator result unverified: from the secondary summary ("barrier management"), no open full text or abstract page reachable]. _Recommendation — add_ one optional coping plan per journey: "If [obstacle], then I will [fallback]". By default the fallback is the **previous, smaller stage**.

**Mental contrasting / WOOP.** _Claim:_ contrasting a wish with its obstacle improves goal pursuit. _Evidence:_ Cross & Sheffield 2019 (MA, k = 7), DOI 10.1080/17437199.2019.1594332, found **g = 0.28** for health behaviour. Adding implementation intentions did not add to the effect ([Taylor & Francis](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17437199.2019.1594332); [Derby](https://repository.derby.ac.uk/item/93z0q/mental-contrasting-for-health-behaviour-change-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-effects-and-moderator-variables)). Wang, Wang & Gai 2021 (MA, 21 studies, N = 15,907), DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202 [verified], found **g = 0.336**, with signs of publication bias ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)). _Replication:_ the evidence base is thin and possibly inflated. _Recommendation — add_ an optional one-minute WOOP at journey creation, whose "Plan" step fills the coping-plan field. Never make it mandatory or repeat it daily.

**Self-monitoring with feedback.** _Claim:_ recording progress improves attainment, most when it is combined with goals and feedback. _Evidence:_ Harkin et al. 2016 (MA, 138 studies, N = 19,951), DOI 10.1037/bul0000025 [verified], found that monitoring frequency mediated goal attainment. Effects were larger when progress was physically recorded or made public ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/); [working paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/1/bul%20harkin%20raw%20final.pdf)). Its goal-attainment effect is d+ = 0.40 (95% CI 0.32–0.48), _Psychol Bull_ 142(2):198–229 [verified, [PubMed 26479070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/)]. Michie et al. 2009 (meta-regression, 122 evaluations), DOI 10.1037/a0016136 (_Health Psychol_ 28(6):690–701) [verified], found an overall effect of 0.31. Combining **self-monitoring with goal setting, feedback or goal review gave 0.42, against 0.26** without ([PubMed 19916637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916637/); [NCBI CRD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK77075/)). _Recommendation — use:_ the one-tap completion of the bound recurring task is the record. **Add** a weekly review that shows stage progress against the target.

**Start tiny, then grade up.** _Claim:_ tiny starting behaviours grow into larger habits (Fogg's B = MAP and Tiny Habits). _Evidence:_ this is a popular claim. Fogg's own evidence is coaching experience ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/02/14/stanford-scientist-on-proven-habits-that-will-make-you-more-productive.html); [Quartz](https://qz.com/877795/how-to-create-new-good-habits-according-to-stanford-psychologist-b-j-fogg)). Fogg 2009, DOI 10.1145/1541948.1541999 [verified, Crossref: _Persuasive '09_, pp. 1–7], is a conceptual paper. A company-reported RCT has no confirmed peer review ([BetterYou](https://www.betteryou.ai/?p=248512), [unverified]). Indirect support is stronger: simpler behaviours automate faster (Lally; Buyalskaya). In addition, **graded tasks** (BCT 8.7) were associated with long-term physical-activity effects in Howlett et al. 2019 (MA), DOI 10.1093/tbm/iby010 [verified]: change d = 0.32, maintenance d = 0.21 ([UHRA](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/handle/2299/20185)). Graded tasks were also a significant moderator in digital interventions in Carraça et al. 2021 (MA), DOI 10.1111/obr.13258 [verified] ([PMC8365685](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8365685/)). _Replication:_ no peer-reviewed RCT of Tiny Habits exists, and no study compares starting tiny with starting at the target dose. _Recommendation — use_ tiny first stages (under two minutes). Keep the **same anchor across stages** so the cue–behaviour link carries over. Never call Tiny Habits "scientifically proven" in copy.

**Identity framing ("I am a walker").** _Claim:_ noun-based identity wording changes behaviour. _Evidence:_ Bryan et al. 2011, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1103343108, reported an 11–14 point gain in turnout ([FORRT](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1073/pnas.1103343108/)). _Replication:_ **failed.** Gerber et al. 2016 found no difference ([PMC5087036](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5087036)). A pre-registered "psychologically authentic" replication in 2020 found that behaviour is unaffected ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/abs/voting-behavior-is-unaffected-by-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence-from-a-psychologically-authentic-replication/7CA9BE69C3213D5030A2BD11A5C53450); [J-PAL](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/voting-behavior-unaffected-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence); critique blog [Replication Index](https://replicationindex.com/2019/12/02/christopher-j-bryan-claims-replicators-p-hack-to-get-non-significant-results-i-claim-he-p-hacked-his-original-results/)). _Recommendation — avoid_ it as a mechanism. It is acceptable as graduation flavour text.

## Lapse handling decides whether a miss becomes a quit

**Tolerant streaks.** _Claim:_ a broken streak lowers later engagement, more so when people blame themselves and less when the streak can be repaired. _Evidence:_ Silverman & Barasch 2023 (7 studies, lab and field), DOI [10.1093/jcr/ucac029](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029) [verified] ([UDel manuscript](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb); [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). In language-app field data, users whose streak broke switched to another language rather than restarting ([CU Boulder news](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). _Replication:_ none was found, and some of the studies use hypothetical scenarios. Grey literature exists: a claim that Duolingo's streak freeze cut churn by 21% has no primary source ([case study](https://jiwonvyoo.github.io/portfolio/case/duolingo/duo.html)), and Busuu reported +15% sessions ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.Com/blog/duolingo-streak-feature-app-engagement-growth)). A 2026 case study finds that streaks drive retention but **do not build app-independent habits** ([IJMAT](https://www.wr-publishing.org/index.php/ijmat/article/view/908)). The underlying engagement claim rests on a qualitative study: Renfree et al. 2016 found that reminders and streaks create dependency and "fragile" habits that collapse when users leave the app ([CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/79500911.pdf); [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/dont-kick-the-habit-the-role-of-dependency-in-habit-formation-app/)). _Recommendation — use_ the tolerant streak, with these details. A single miss shows as a neutral hollow dot, never a red break. When a run does end, show the total and best runs, never "reset to 0". The break screen uses no-blame copy. **Avoid** purchasable freezes.

**What-the-hell and abstinence-violation effects.** _Claim:_ perceiving that a rigid rule has been broken triggers giving up. _Evidence:_ classic small lab studies, all DOIs [verified] in Europe PMC (PubMed 1206453, 3857016, 20691231, 3571666). They include Herman & Mack 1975 (10.1111/j.1467-6494.1975.tb00727.x), Polivy & Herman 1985 (10.1037/0003-066X.40.2.193) and Polivy, Herman & Deo 2010 (10.1016/j.appet.2010.07.015), in which merely _believing_ a slice was large was enough ([PsyBlog](https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=13881); [overview](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Counterregulatory%20eating)). Curry, Marlatt & Gordon 1987 (10.1037/0022-006X.55.2.145) is a correlational smoking study. Some people respond to a lapse with course correction instead ([Phys.org on Zemack-Rugar 2014](https://phys.org/news/2014-05-consumer-behavior-response-discipline-key.pdf)). _Replication:_ no modern pre-registered replication exists, and a critique points to Tomiyama et al. 2009, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02436.x [verified]: in two everyday-life studies, "dieters did not overeat following violations of their diet" ([PMC2761536](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2761536/)). _Recommendation — use_ "never miss twice" and self-recovering HP as countermeasures. **Avoid** "perfect week" badges, "0/7" displays and trait language such as "you're inconsistent".

**Self-compassion.** _Claim:_ kindness after failure increases the motivation to try again. _Evidence:_

| Adams & Leary 2007             | Lab study                       | Self-compassion after a preload reduced later eating among restrictive eaters              | [verified]                                                                          | [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/782823)                   |

An "Adriaanse 2014" paper on this topic does not exist: the only Adriaanse et al. 2014 in Crossref is about habit mediating self-control and snacking (10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00444), not self-compassion or lapses [verified, Crossref search]; do not cite it. _Replication:_ the evidence comes from small samples, and no large replications were found. _Recommendation — use_ self-compassionate copy paired with a concrete next step, for example "Missing a day is normal. What's one small step today?". Render low HP as "resting", never "dying".

**Emergency reserves.** _Claim:_ a challenging goal with a limited, slightly costly reserve of skips beats both easy and rigid goals. _Evidence:_ Sharif & Shu 2017 (multi-study, lab and field), DOI [10.1509/jmr.15.0231](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.15.0231) [verified] ([UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/); [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Designing-More-Effective-Goals-by-Using-Emergency-Reserves-A-Field-Experiment.pdf)). Sharif & Shu 2021 (OBHDP 163) [verified] found that reserves aid persistence after a subgoal fails ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:163:y:2021:i:c:p:17-29); [author PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf)). _Replication:_ both papers come from the same authors. _Recommendation:_ "never miss twice" already acts as a bounded reserve, so **keep it bounded**. Do not add unlimited free skips. A pause or sick mode is a different thing and should stay available.

**Comeback acknowledgement.** _Claim:_ rewarding a return after a miss is the most effective single mechanic tested for exercise. _Evidence:_ Milkman et al. 2021 (Mega: 61,293 members of a US fitness chain, 54 four-week programmes), DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4 [verified against the full text, [PMC8822539](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)]. **45% of the programmes raised weekly gym visits by 9–27%** against a placebo control that visited 1.48 times a week; none reduced visits. The top programme gave a bonus of 125 points (**US$0.09**) for returning to the gym after a missed workout: **+0.40 weekly visits, a 27% increase over placebo**, and **16% over the planning, reminders and micro-incentives condition** (b = 0.27, P = 0.010). The two figures in the press are therefore both right, against different comparison groups. **Only 8% of programmes had effects that were significant and measurable after the four weeks**, and forecasts by impartial judges failed to predict which programmes would work. A blog analysis questions the multiple comparisons ([jcx.au](https://blog.jcx.au/posts/megastudy-scepticism)). _Replication:_ one megastudy, not yet replicated. _Recommendation — add_ a deterministic comeback moment on the completion after a miss. In calm mode it is a warm line: "Comeback — arc kept." In RPG mode it is a fixed bonus guardian hit or story beat. Misses are defined against the user's **own planned schedule** from the recurring task, as in the winning arm.

**Fresh starts.** _Claim:_ temporal landmarks prompt aspirational behaviour. _Evidence:_ Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 (archival), DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 [verified] ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901); [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)). Beshears et al. 2021 (Field exp., N = 6,082), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2021.06.005 [verified], found that fresh-start framing raised retirement savings, while placebo dates and New Year framing did not ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:167:y:2021:i:c:p:72-87); [UCLA Anderson](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/fresh-start-framing-boosts-retirement-plan-participation/); [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news-info/daily-news/how-fresh-start-framing-can-boost-retirement-savings)). A 2026 dissertation (not peer-reviewed) reports that the effect appears only for **self-chosen** start dates ([eGrove](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/etd/2026)). Koo, Dai et al. (in press, status unconfirmed) report that an upcoming landmark licenses slacking on goals already under way ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson-review.ucla.edu/fresh-start)). _Replication:_ no multi-lab replication. _Recommendation — add_ self-chosen start dates (Monday, the 1st, a birthday) at journey creation, and landmark restarts after a run has ended. **Avoid** "start fresh next week" prompts to users who are actively progressing.

## The game layer works modestly, so keep it deterministic and optional

**Autonomy first (self-determination theory).** _Claim:_ need-supportive, autonomous motivation predicts health behaviour, and interventions built on it work. _Evidence:_ Ng et al. 2012 (MA, 184 data sets, mostly correlational), DOI 10.1177/1745691612447309. The DOI and data-set count are [verified] ([PubMed 26168470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26168470/)). The pooled correlations are [verified, full text] ([SDT PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2012-NgNtoumanis_PPS.pdf)): need satisfaction and autonomous regulation correlated with positive mental health at ρ = .22 to .62, with negative mental health at ρ = −.05 to −.50, and with physical-health indices at ρ = .07 to .67; controlled regulation and amotivation went the other way. The weakest links were need satisfaction with healthy diet (ρ = .07 to .14), and the data are mostly cross-sectional ([Ewha](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/self-determination-theory-applied-to-health-contexts-a-meta-analy/)). Ntoumanis et al. 2021 (MA, 73 studies), DOI 10.1080/17437199.2020.1718529 [verified abstract], found small positive effects ([Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-self-determination-theory-informed-interventio/); [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/155741/)). Sheeran et al. 2020 (MA of 65 tests from RCTs), DOI **10.1037/ccp0000501** (corrected; 10.1037/ccp0000485 is an unrelated comment by Siddaway et al.) [verified, [PubMed 32437175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32437175/)], found **d+ = 0.23, still significant after bias correction (d+ ≥ .15)** ([White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/164092/3/SDT%20ABM%202020.pdf)). _Replication:_ this is the most robust motivational base in the review, though effects are small and measures self-reported. _Recommendation — use:_ keep calm mode as the default, RPG as an explicit opt-in, user-chosen journeys and targets, and penalty-free pause. Calm mode must be a full long-term state, not a downgrade.

A popular source cites different Mazeas figures (+1,421 steps/day, g = 0.58) ([getfitcraft](https://getfitcraft.com/science/gamification-statistics)). Both appear in the paper but measure something else: +1,420.57 steps/day is the estimate before two studies were excluded, and g = 0.58 is the effect against inactive controls only (against active controls g = 0.23) [verified, PMC8767479]. At follow-up (12–24 weeks after the intervention) the effect was **not significant across all studies (g = 0.09, CI −0.07 to 0.26)** and **g = 0.15 (0.07–0.23) after the sensitivity analysis**; the authors read this as persistence, but it is small and depends on excluding studies [verified].

_Replication:_ effects are heterogeneous, fade with novelty, and no study isolates _opt-in_ gamification. _Recommendation — use_ the RPG layer as optional, low-stakes feedback and expect 0.2–0.4 SD while it is active. Late journey stages should need less game scaffolding.

**XP, levels and the undermining effect.** _Claim:_ expected, tangible, contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, while informational feedback and praise do not. _Evidence:_ Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (MA, 128 experiments), DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627 [verified for contingency effects]: engagement-contingent rewards d = −0.40, completion-contingent −0.36, performance-contingent −0.28 ([PubMed 10589297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/); [UW PDF](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)). Positive feedback (verbal rewards) enhanced free-choice behaviour (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31) [verified, abstract]. All tangible rewards and all expected rewards undermined motivation [verified, abstract]; the tangible-reward value d = −0.34 (free choice, 92 studies) and the finding that unexpected rewards did not undermine (d = 0.01, 9 studies) are [verified, the authors' 2001 summary of the 1999 results, [SDT PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf), Table 1]. Cameron & Pierce 1994 (DOI 10.3102/00346543064003363 [verified, Crossref: _Rev Educ Res_ 64(3):363–423]) disputed the conclusion. Cameron, Banko & Pierce 2001 ([PMC2731358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2731358/)) conceded harm for high-interest tasks with expected, tangible rewards loosely tied to performance ([Ewha](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/extrinsic-rewards-and-intrinsic-motivation-in-education-reconside/); [Deci et al. 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001); [SDT PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)). _Replication:_ the debate is long-standing, and **no experiment tests whether virtual XP undermines motivation like tangible rewards**. _Recommendation — use_ fixed XP per completion and cosmetic-only levels, framed informationally ("20 walks done"). **Avoid** announcing XP before tasks the user already enjoys, and avoid XP for time-on-task. Turning RPG off must never feel like losing everything.

**Guardians, badges and leaderboards.** _Claim:_ social structure carries the behavioural effect, and leaderboards demotivate low performers. _Evidence:_ Patel et al. 2019, STEP UP (RCT, n = 602), DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.3505 [verified, [PMC6735420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6735420/)]. Compared with control, **competition added 920 steps/day, support 689 and collaboration 637**. **Only competition persisted after the game ended (+569).** The trial was funded by Deloitte and the Penn Nudge Unit ([PubMed 31498375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31498375/); [Penn Medicine](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/901675)). It appeared in _JAMA Intern Med_ **179(12)**:1624–1632 [verified]. BE FIT 2017 (DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.3458, _JAMA Intern Med_ 177(11):1586–1593) is [verified] ([PMC5710273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710273/)). Leaderboard studies are mixed: low ranks demotivate some users, while other studies report more effort ([Emerald 2023](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-12-2021-0897/full/html); [ScholarSpace](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/70776); [arXiv 1707.03704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03704v1); [PMC4698889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4698889); [Jia 2018](https://scholarworks.iupui.edu/bitstream/1805/17645/1/Jia_2018_designing.pdf); [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000169056/152385336); [Warwick](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/173148/)). "Social incentive" was a significant moderator in digital interventions (Carraça 2021). _Recommendation:_ **keep no leaderboards**, and document it internally as a wellbeing and values choice: competition was the strongest arm. **Use** guardians as collaborative opponents worn down by completions, never punishing misses. **Use** badges only as stage milestones. **Add** an opt-in supporter or party with a weekly digest (the support arm), and apply "never miss twice" to the party so that one member cannot "break" it.

**Self-recovering HP and loss framing.** _Claim:_ loss framing boosts behaviour during an intervention but not afterwards, and punishment mechanics backfire. _Evidence:_ Patel et al. 2016 (RCT, n = 281), DOI [10.7326/M15-1635](https://doi.org/10.7326/M15-1635) [verified]. Only the loss arm beat control (45% vs 30% of days at goal), and **nothing persisted** after incentives ended ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/2016-03-24/research/prospect-losing-money-pushes-people-achieve-fitness-goals); [NCT02030119](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02030119)). Diefenbach & Müssig 2019 (mixed methods, field study n = 45), DOI [10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004) [verified], found that **all Habitica participants experienced counterproductive effects**, such as being punished during productive periods ([LMU](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77668)). _Recommendation — use_ HP only as informational (last seven days), self-healing and optional. **Add** a backfill window ("done yesterday") and a pause/sick mode. **Avoid** item loss, death or resets.

**Goal gradient and endowed progress.** _Claim:_ effort accelerates near a goal, and a head start speeds completion. _Evidence:_ Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, DOI 10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39 [verified], found that gaps between coffee purchases shrank by 20% (0.7 days) on average as members neared the free coffee ([Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf); [Columbia summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration); discrepant blog figures at [Alfred](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/goal-gradient-effect)). After the first reward, purchase rates **reset to a slower pace and then accelerated again** toward the second reward: among 110 members who completed a second card, its first two gaps (3.1 and 2.7 days) were slower than the last two on the first card (2.2 and 2.1 days; p < .01) [verified, Columbia PDF]. Nunes & Drèze 2006, DOI 10.1086/500480 [DOI verified, Crossref: _J Consum Res_ 32(4):504–512; completion figures unverified: secondary sources only; the author copy on msbfile03.usc.edu is refused by this environment's proxy], found ~19% vs ~34% completion. The progress principle (Amabile & Kramer 2011) is a correlational diary study [unverified: book; its existence is confirmed by reviews in Crossref, its design was not checked, and hbr.org and hbs.edu are refused by this environment's proxy] ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins)). _Replication:_ none independent. _Recommendation — use_ short stages and show the distance to the _next_ stage. When a stage or guardian falls, immediately show the next stage's first step. Endowed progress is acceptable only if it is honest ("setup counts as step one").

**Narrative.** _Claim:_ story improves adherence. _Evidence:_ game fiction was a moderator in Sailer & Homner, based on few studies. Schwarz et al. 2021 (cluster RCT), DOI 10.3390/ijerph18147444 [verified], found low engagement ([PMC8305797](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8305797/)). In a 2025 medication-app pilot, storytelling "was not favored" ([PMC11781755](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11781755/)). Transportation theory is [verified] (Green & Brock 2000, 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701, _J Pers Soc Psychol_ 79(5):701–721, [PubMed 11079236](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/)); its meta-analysis is [verified, Crossref: _J Consum Res_ 40(5):797–817] (van Laer 2014, 10.1086/673383). The claim that Zombies, Run! improves adherence is a popular claim. _Recommendation — use lightly:_ short, optional, unexpected story beats, never a gate before logging.

**Temptation bundling.** _Claim:_ pairing a treat with the habit raises it. _Evidence:_ Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2013.1784 [verified], found **+51%** gym visits at first, decaying over time ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25843979/); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1784)). The larger Kirgios et al. 2020 (N = 6,792, a four-week program), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2020.09.003 [DOI verified, Crossref: _Organ Behav Hum Decis Process_ 161:20–35], found that an audiobook plus encouragement to bundle raised **average weekly workouts by 10–12%** (the chance of a weekly workout by 10–14%) during the program and up to 17 weeks after it [verified, Wharton PDF]. Teaching bundling added only a modest effect on whether people worked out at all beyond giving the audiobook ([Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/TemptationBundling.pdf); [commentary](https://beoutlines.blogspot.com/2023/08/kirgios-mandel-park-et-al-2020-on.html); [Rotman](https://www.pressreader.com/canada/rotman-management-magazine/20210501/282973927858878)). _Replication:_ replicated at scale with a much smaller effect. _Recommendation — add_ an optional "pair it with" field per stage, shown in the plan sentence. Never lock the treat behind an in-app reward.

**Commitment devices.** Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010 (review), DOI 10.1146/annurev.economics.102308.124324 [DOI verified, Crossref: _Annu Rev Econ_ 2:671–698], finds low take-up and forfeited stakes [unverified: not in the abstract; LSE Research Online, NBER and Yale pages are refused by this environment's proxy]. _Recommendation — avoid_ money contracts. **Add** at most a soft, self-written one-line promise.

**Random rewards, login bonuses and dark patterns.** _Claim:_ variable-ratio rewards drive compulsive use. _Evidence:_ the lab basis is Ferster & Skinner 1957, _Schedules of Reinforcement_, 10.1037/10627-000 [verified, Crossref]. **No RCT shows that random rewards improve health outcomes.** Loot boxes correlate with problem gambling (Zendle & Cairns 2018, a large-scale survey, 10.1371/journal.pone.0206767 [verified], [PMC6248934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6248934/)). The relevant ethics and law are as follows, all DOIs [verified, Crossref] unless marked:

- Berdichevsky & Neuenschwander 1999, 10.1145/301353.301410 (_Commun ACM_ 42(5):51–58): disclose persuasion and use none the user would not consent to.
- Fogg 2003 on the ethics of persuasive technology, a chapter of _Persuasive Technology_ (pp. 211–239), 10.1016/B978-155860643-2/50011-1.
- Gray et al. 2018 on dark patterns, 10.1145/3173574.3174108 (_Proc. CHI 2018_).
- Mathur et al. 2019 on dark patterns at scale, 10.1145/3359183 (_Proc. ACM HCI_ 3(CSCW)).
- EU DSA Art. 25 bans manipulative interfaces on online platforms ([verified], [text](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/de-de/out-law/analyse/dark-patterns-digital-regulation-consumer-law)). A standalone habit app is likely not an "online platform", but the UCPD applies.
- The planned Digital Fairness Act targets "penalties for disengagement". As of 5 Nov 2026 the Commission had **not yet proposed it**: the EP Legislative Train (updated 25 Oct 2026) lists it as "Announced", indicative date Q4 2026, "scheduled for mid-November", targeting dark patterns and addictive design [verified] (EPRS briefing of October 2026, [EPRS](<https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/EPRS_BRI(2026)791529>)); tabling on 11 Nov 2026 is reported by [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/campaigns/tech-data-and-ai-the-digital-frontier/eu-digital-strategy/navigating-the-eus-digital-fairness-act) [unverified: the exact date is not on the EU page] ([EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act); [Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102m222/digital-fairness-act-european-commission-publishes-responses-to-consultation); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/08/01/eu-consults-on-digital-fairness-act-big-changes-ahead-for-consumer-facing-platforms/)).

_Recommendation — keep_ no random rewards and no login bonuses. **Avoid** confirmshaming, streak-loss threat notifications and obstructed deletion. **Add** a "How QuestArc motivates you" page.

**Reminders.** _Claim:_ reminders support repetition but hinder automaticity. _Evidence:_ Stawarz 2015 (small field study; sample size [unverified: the CHI paper is not in Europe PMC and Crossref has no abstract; UCL Discovery (eprint 1468224) and Cardiff ORCA (130626) answer with a bot check, and CORE and Bristol's repository are refused by this environment's proxy]) [verified finding]. A 2016 medication follow-up abstract found that reminder users forgot less often but reported low automaticity ([Frontiers abstract](https://www.frontiersin.org/10.3389/conf.FPUBH.2016.01.00066/event_abstract); later paper [UCL PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1522125/7/Stawarz_2055207616678707.pdf)). _Replication:_ small samples, and no RCT of fading schedules exists. _Recommendation — use_ reminders that restate the plan ("After lunch → walk 5 min"), timed to the anchor. **Fade** them as completion and automaticity rise.

**Success metric.** Yardley et al. 2016 (10.1016/j.amepre.2016.06.015 [verified], [PubMed 27745683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27745683/)) separates "effective engagement" with the behaviour from use of the app. Eysenbach 2005 (10.2196/jmir.7.1.e11 [verified], [PMC1550631](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1550631/)) describes the "law of attrition". Laranjo et al. 2021 (MA, 28 studies) found that apps and trackers added ~1,850 steps/day (95% CI 1,247–2,457), with high heterogeneity (I² = 69%) ([BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/2021/01/26/do-smartphone-applications-and-activity-trackers-increase-physical-activity-in-adults/); [NOVA PDF](https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/147424/1/422.full.pdf); DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102892 [verified], [PubMed 33355160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33355160/)). _Recommendation — measure_ completion over 8–12 weeks plus SRBAI. Do not measure daily active users or streak length.

## Quotes do nothing measurable; the user's own "why" and actionable nuggets do

**Generic motivational quotes.** _Claim:_ inspirational quotes motivate behaviour. _Evidence:_ **this is a popular claim, and no experiment was found.** Trait inspiration and inspiration about one's own goal predict goal progress correlationally (Milyavskaya et al. 2012, 10.1016/j.paid.2011.08.031; sample sizes and effects not retrieved, [reference list](https://typeset.io/pdf/nostalgia-evoked-inspiration-mediating-mechanisms-and-cenra9swi8.pdf)). Thrash & Elliot 2003 (10.1037/0022-3514.84.4.871 [verified], [PubMed 12703654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12703654/)) describes inspiration as something that _happens to_ a person and cannot be produced on demand. Web claims are untraceable ([example](https://ischoolconnect.com/blog/?p=16279)). The feared harm also fails: Wood et al. 2009 (10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02370.x, seen only on a [secondary page](https://studiessuggest.pages.dev/stories/positive-affirmations-backfire-low-self-esteem.html)) reported that positive self-statements backfire for people with low self-esteem. **The replication failed:** Flynn & Bordieri 2020 found no effects on mood, self-esteem or goal completion ([ACBS](https://contextualscience.org/publications/flynn_bordieri_2020); [abstract](https://contextualscience.org/node/29886)). The real risks of quotes are irrelevance, wear-out and preachiness. _Recommendation — avoid_ a daily quote feature. If quotes stay for RPG flavour, they should be off by default, appear only at milestones, be non-directive, and never be pushed.

**The user's own "why".** _Claim:_ reflecting on one's own values improves health behaviour. _Evidence:_ Epton et al. 2015 (MA, 144 tests), DOI 10.1037/hea0000116 [verified]: acceptance d = 0.17, intentions d = 0.14, **behaviour d = 0.32** ([PubMed 25133846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25133846/); [Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/the-impact-of-self-affirmation-on-health-behavior-change-a-meta-a/)). A second 2015 meta-analysis found behaviour d+ ≈ 0.27; its authorship is unconfirmed, likely Sweeney & Moyer ([Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/self-affirmation-and-responses-to-health-messages-a-meta-analysis/)). Values writing did not change goal completion in Flynn & Bordieri Study 2. _Caveat:_ self-affirmation mainly reduces defensiveness to threatening messages, so the transfer to "own reasons in own words" is supported by SDT rather than shown directly. _Recommendation — use:_ ask once per journey (optionally) "Why does this matter to you?". Show it back at stations and after a miss.

**Learning nuggets.** _Claim:_ brief habit-science advice changes behaviour. _Evidence:_ the Ten Top Tips RCT is Beeken et al. 2017 (n = 537). It found **0.87 kg more weight lost at 3 months**; by 24 months the control group had caught up; cost ~£23 per person ([UCL PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1530679/1/ijo2016206a.pdf); [ISRCTN16347068](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN16347068)). The intervention was a package of _actionable_ tips plus self-monitoring, built on Gardner, Lally & Wardle 2012 (10.3399/bjgp12X659466 [verified], [PMC3505409](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3505409/)). For microlearning, a meta-analysis in higher education ([JMTR PDF](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/08/28/jmtr_24_22.pdf)) and De Gagne 2019 ([secondary](https://www.visualsp.com/blog/8-studies-that-prove-microlearning-cant-be-ignored/)) show knowledge gains, not behaviour change. Vendor figures are unsourced ([Arist](https://www.arist.co/post/2025-meta-analysis-microlearning-improves-job-behavior-50-percent/blogrelixir)). Spacing is robust: distributed practice received a "high utility" rating (Dunlosky 2013, 10.1177/1529100612453266 [verified], [PubMed 26173288](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)). Messaging meta-analyses also bear on delivery:

| Noar et al. 2007               | MA, tailored print interventions              | Tailoring **r = .074**                                                                    | DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.133.4.673, [PubMed 17592961](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17592961/)                                                                         |
| Orr & King 2015; Fjeldsoe 2009 | MA, 38 RCTs of SMS; review, 14 studies        | g = 0.291, larger with several messages a day (0.395 vs 0.244); 13 of 14 studies positive | [verified], [PubMed 25739668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25739668/); [PubMed 19135907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19135907/)                                |

_Recommendation — add_ one short, actionable "try this" nugget per station, matched to the stage. A suggested sequence: choose a reliable cue → shrink the action → plan for a missed day → one miss is fine → pair it with something you like → you will need the app less. Re-show the "one miss is fine" nugget after a miss (spaced, at the moment of need). Never use trivia without an action, and never repeat nuggets otherwise.

**Timing, tone and fatigue.** _Timing:_ HeartSteps (micro-randomized trial; Klasnja 2019) found that contextual suggestions raised steps by **167 (66%) in the next 30 minutes, an effect that faded over six weeks**; generic anti-sedentary messages did nothing ([PMC6401341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341); [PubMed 30192907](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30192907/); reanalyses [arXiv 2502.13500](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13500), [arXiv 2410.15049](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15049)). Just-in-time adaptive intervention design treats "deliver nothing" as a valid option and delivers only when the user is receptive (Nahum-Shani 2018, 10.1007/s12160-016-9830-8, [PMC5364076](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364076); [Künzler](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/dfk/research/kunzler-receptivity/kunzler-receptivity.pdf)). _Tone:_ controlling language provokes reactance (Rains 2013 MA, 10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x, [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093682); Miller et al. 2007, [via PMC6393822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6393822/); Stok & Renner 2017, [EHPS](https://ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/view/2586)). One trial found no effect of autonomy-supportive wording (Altendorf 2019, [Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/should-or-could-testing-the-use-of-autonomy-supportive-language-a/)). A 2026 meta-analysis reports the reactance cost is small but accumulates ([PsyPost, unverified](https://www.psypost.org/forceful-language-makes-people-resist-health-advice/)). Dillard & Shen 2005, 10.1080/03637750500111815 (_Commun Monogr_ 72(2):144–168) is [verified, Crossref]; its content was not checked. _Fatigue:_ repetition causes avoidance through overexposure, redundancy, exhaustion and boredom (So, Kim & Cohen 2017, [Comm. Monographs](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/03637751.2016.1250429); [PMC5772987](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5772987); [review](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/no-more-messages-a-systematic-review-of-predictors-and-outcomes-o/)). Stawarz 2015 found **positive reinforcement had no effect on automaticity**, so celebratory messages are for enjoyment, not habit building. _How:_ show messages in the app at receptive moments: station reached, stage choice, after a miss. That fits both hosts, since the Super Productivity plugin and the Linux phone app can surface them where the task is completed, without push. Use wording such as "you could" and "many people find", make every message dismissible, and use fewer as stages progress.

## Focused work: evidence for the journey First Things First

Checked on 2026-11-06 for [First Things First](journey-first-things-first.md) (#76) against Crossref metadata, Europe PMC abstracts, PMC full texts, the HBR article page and author-hosted PDFs on interruptions.net. Publisher, SSRN and ACM pages were refused by this environment's proxy; HBS answered automated requests with HTTP 403. Details known only from secondary sources are marked [unverified]. In short: the stages are well chosen, but most of the evidence supports **calmer work**, not **more output**.

**One task written down on sitting down.** _Claim:_ an if-then plan tied to sitting down at the desk raises the chance of starting the key task. _Evidence:_ Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (see Implementation intentions above), DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1 [verified, Crossref]. Leroy & Glomb 2018 (four studies), DOI 10.1287/orsc.2017.1184 [verified, Crossref abstract]: a "ready-to-resume" plan (briefly planning the return to an interrupted task) reduced attention residue; sample and effect sizes are not in the abstract. _Replication:_ the bias-corrected effect of implementation intentions is small (Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, above). Choosing **one** most important task ("eat the frog") is a popular heuristic; no study comparing it with a ranked list was found. _Recommendation — use_ the plan sentence and expect a small effect; _avoid_ productivity figures.

**Task-switching costs.** _Claim:_ switching between tasks costs time; preparation reduces but does not remove the cost. _Evidence:_ Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans 2001 (four lab experiments), DOI 10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763 [verified, Crossref; abstract in Europe PMC, PubMed 11518143]: switching-time costs rose with rule complexity and fell with task cuing. Monsell 2003 (review), DOI 10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7 [verified, Crossref; abstract in Europe PMC, PubMed 12639695]: responses are slower and usually more error-prone right after a switch; the cost "is reduced, but not eliminated, by an opportunity for preparation". _Replication:_ one of the most replicated effects in cognitive psychology, but measured on simple lab tasks over seconds; neither paper gives minutes of office work. The "40% of productive time" figure comes from an APA web article, not from these papers [unverified]. _Recommendation — use_ as the reason for "one task first, messages after"; _avoid_ minute or percentage figures.

**Attention residue.** _Claim:_ thoughts about an unfinished task linger and lower performance on the next one. _Evidence:_ Leroy 2009 (two lab experiments), DOI 10.1016/j.obhdp.2009.04.002 [verified, Crossref; no abstract there]. Per the abstract as quoted in search results, finishing a task before switching is "not enough"; time pressure while finishing helped [unverified: snippets only]. _Replication:_ follow-up work is by the same author; no independent replication found. _Recommendation — use_ "write it on a parking list and go back to your task" as a nugget; _avoid_ a fixed duration of residue.

**Email in blocks.** _Claim:_ checking email a few times a day lowers stress. _Evidence:_ Kushlev & Dunn 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2014.11.005 [verified, full text via [interruptions.net](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-ComputHumBehav15.pdf)]: a two-week, counterbalanced within-subject field experiment with 124 adults (142 enrolled, 18 dropped out; about two-thirds students, recruited as people who felt overwhelmed by email). In one week they were asked to check email three times a day with alerts off; in the other, as often as they liked with the inbox open. Daily stress was lower in the limited week (F(1, 121) = 4.18, p = .04, d = .37); tension during one important activity was lower (d = .45), and people felt less distracted by email (d = .51). Stress recalled for the whole week did not differ, and self-rated productivity showed no direct effect ("No other significant main effects emerged"). _Replication:_ a single study; compliance was imperfect (4.7 self-reported checks a day in the limited week, 12.5 in the unlimited week, 15.5 at baseline); all outcomes are self-reports; there was no control week. The authors call it exploratory: the stress effect may be "an artifact of the large number of statistical tests". _Recommendation — use_ email and chat blocks as a calm habit and let the user choose how many; _avoid_ promising more productivity, which the study did not find.

**Reflecting on yesterday.** _Claim:_ short written reflection on recent work improves later performance. _Evidence:_ Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats, HBS working paper 14-093 [unverified: HBS returned HTTP 403 to automated requests]. SSRN version "Learning by Thinking", DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2414478 [verified, Crossref abstract]: ten experimental studies (N = 4,340); reflection helps mostly early on the learning curve. A conference abstract exists (Di Stefano, Pisano & Staats, Academy of Management Proceedings 2015, DOI 10.5465/ambpp.2015.12709abstract [verified, Crossref; no abstract there]). No peer-reviewed journal version was found. The field experiment (101 trainee agents at Wipro, 15 minutes of reflection instead of practice) is reported as about 21% better test performance [unverified: secondary summaries; the often quoted "23%" appeared in no source]. _Replication:_ no independent field replication; the task was learning a new job. _Recommendation — use_ a one-line note on yesterday's progress; _avoid_ quoting any percentage.

**Small wins.** _Claim:_ progress in meaningful work goes with good days at work. _Evidence:_ Amabile & Kramer 2011, ["The power of small wins"](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins), Harvard Business Review, May 2011 [verified, hbr.org; no DOI], and _The Progress Principle_. Diary study: 238 members of 26 project teams in seven companies answered an end-of-day email survey for "just over four months, on average", giving "nearly 12,000 diary entries". Steps forward occurred on 76% of the best-mood days, setbacks on 13%. _Replication:_ correlational; the authors write that their "analyses establish correlations but do not prove causality" and that "the causality goes both ways". The study does not test whether recording progress causes anything; causal support for recording comes from self-monitoring (Harkin et al. 2016, above). _Recommendation — use_ the progress note framed as noticing; _avoid_ "proven to boost motivation".

**Time management helps well-being more than performance.** _Evidence:_ Aeon, Faber & Panaccio 2021 (MA, 158 studies, 490 effect sizes), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0245066, full text PMC7799745 [verified]: time management correlates r = .26 with overall job performance (k = 21, N = 3,990), r = .31 with overall well-being (k = 30, N = 9,905), r = .43 with life satisfaction and r = −.22 with distress. Most studies are cross-sectional and heterogeneity is high. _Recommendation — use_ calm framing ("a focused start of your workday"); _avoid_ selling the journey as a productivity booster.

**Micro-breaks.** _Evidence:_ Albulescu et al. 2022 (MA, 22 samples, N = 2,335), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0272460, PMC9432722 [verified]: small effects on vigour (d = .36) and fatigue (d = .35); the effect on performance was not significant (d = .16) and appeared only for less demanding tasks. _Recommendation:_ a break between blocks may be suggested for energy, never as a performance boost.

**Popular claims to keep out of the texts.**

- _Pomodoro as an evidence-based method:_ Biwer et al. 2023 (87 students), DOI 10.1111/bjep.12593 [verified, Europe PMC abstract]: systematic breaks gave less fatigue and more concentration but no difference in effort or task completion; two later small student studies from the same group (DOI 10.3390/bs15070861, 10.3390/bs16071158 [verified, Crossref abstracts]) found no performance advantage. No workplace trial.
- _90-minute ultradian work cycles:_ Kleitman's rest–activity cycle is established for sleep; daytime evidence is thin (Hayashi, Sato & Hori 1994, ten students, DOI 10.2466/pms.1994.79.2.791 [verified], found cycles of about two hours). No workplace trial.
- _"23 minutes to refocus":_ traced to a 2006 interview with Gloria Mark, not a paper [unverified]. Mark, Gonzalez & Harris 2005, DOI 10.1145/1054972.1055017 [verified, author PDF via [interruptions.net](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI05-p321-mark.pdf)], observed information workers for over 700 hours: 57% of their activities were interrupted, and work resumed on the same day was taken up again after an average of 25 min 26 s (SD 54 min 48 s), with about two other activities in between. That is the time until return, not the time needed to refocus, and the spread is very large.

_Recommendation for First Things First:_ the 15 → 45 minutes are a gentle ramp the user may change, not an optimal duration; texts promise no output, no minutes and no percentages; the best-supported benefits are getting started (if-then plan) and less stress (email blocks).

## What changes in QuestArc's design rules

The evidence turns into ten concrete rule changes:

1. **Graduation is gated on automaticity, not days.** A habit graduates when the one-item SRBAI stays high (for example ≥ 5–6 of 7 for two to three consecutive weekly check-ins), completion is consistent, and reminders have already faded without a drop. The threshold is a design choice. Drop any "66 days" trigger, including the one suggested in one note. Add check-ins at two and six weeks after graduation and a one-tap "bring it back". Keep graduated habits visible as achievements.
2. **A comeback acknowledgement after a miss.** The first completion after a miss gets a fixed, non-random acknowledgement: a line in calm mode, a bonus hit in RPG mode.
3. **A temptation-bundling field.** Each stage gets an optional "pair it with" field, shown in the plan sentence.
4. **A weekly automaticity check-in** with the validated single item ("Behaviour X is something I do automatically", 1–7). It feeds graduation, stage advancement and reminder fading.
5. **The fallback plan is the previous, smaller stage.** It is the default coping plan; stepping back carries no penalty, and the second consecutive miss offers it.
6. **Self-chosen start dates** (today, Monday, the 1st, a chosen date) at journey creation, and landmark restarts only after a run has ended.
7. **The user's own "why" replaces quotes.** It is captured once and shown at stations and after misses. Quotes are off by default, if they exist at all.
8. **Reminders restate the plan and fade:** daily, then weekly, then off as automaticity rises, with an explicit message such as "you won't need me for this one".
9. **Plus smaller rules:**
   - the plan is stored as anchor, action and place, and either a routine or a time anchor is allowed;
   - misses are defined against the recurring task's own schedule;
   - a backfill window ("done yesterday") and a pause/sick mode;
   - a neutral gap display, plus total and best runs instead of a reset;
   - no-blame, autonomy-supportive copy throughout;
   - optional WOOP and supporter;
   - nuggets in the app at receptive moments only;
   - a "How QuestArc motivates you" disclosure page.
10. **The success metric is 8–12-week completion plus SRBAI**, never app opens.

## Open evidence gaps

The largest gap is QuestArc's signature: **no study measures behaviour after tracking and reminders are deliberately removed once automaticity is high**. Second, no study tests multi-stage journeys in which the behaviour grows. It is unknown whether the automaticity of "walk 5 minutes" transfers to "walk 15 minutes". No validated SRBAI cut-off for "formed" exists. No study compares tolerating zero, one or two consecutive misses. No study tests whether virtual XP undermines intrinsic motivation, compares opt-in with mandatory gamification, or isolates narrative in a habit app. No RCT tests reminder-fading schedules, self-compassion copy inside an app, or quotes and nuggets against behaviour.

Several studies the design leans on are unreplicated single papers: Dalton & Spiller, Verhoeven, goal gradient, endowed progress, Silverman & Barasch, Sharif & Shu and the Milkman top arm. Specific figures need checking against primary texts:

- the Bélanger-Gravel g values and coping-plan moderator (DOI confirmed; no open full text or abstract page reachable);
- the Keller 23% figure (no open full text in Europe PMC; Refubium behind a bot check, Minerva Access refused);
- the Stawarz 2015 sample size (UCL Discovery and Cardiff ORCA behind bot checks);
- the Nunes & Drèze completion figures, the Bryan, Karlan & Nelson take-up finding and the Amabile & Kramer study design (DOIs or books confirmed; the open copies found are on hosts this environment refuses).

The Kivetz post-reward resetting, the Kirgios figures and the Ng 2012 pooled correlations are now checked against the full texts.

All DOIs in this report now match Crossref. The 2024 implementation-intention update is verified and gives smaller effects for behaviour (d = .27) and physical activity (d = .14) than the 2006 d = .65.

The Digital Fairness Act was still only announced on 25 Oct 2026, with a proposal expected in mid-November 2026; its status and the DSA Article 25 guidelines must be re-checked at publication. QuestArc can fill some of these gaps itself, ethically, with opt-in A/B tests of story beats, comeback acknowledgements and reminder fading, measured by 8–12-week completion and SRBAI.

## Conclusion

QuestArc's design is closer to the evidence than most habit apps. Its strongest choices are calm defaults, a single journey, tolerant lapses and no random rewards. These are not the "fun" parts; they are the parts that keep a miss from turning into a quit and keep the app from becoming the cue. The literature points to a reframing: the RPG layer is a temporary scaffold whose job is to hand off to context-cued automaticity, and the app's measure of success is how little the user needs it by the last stage. That makes graduation on measured automaticity the feature most worth building well, and also the one QuestArc is best placed to generate evidence for.

Two choices are value judgements rather than evidence-maximising ones, and the team should own them as such. Excluding competition gives up the one social mechanic whose effect persisted in STEP UP. Avoiding quotes in favour of the user's own words trades familiar app "feel" for the only motivational-message approach with meta-analytic support.
