# Journey: Tidy a little

Issue #37. The owner chose the topic: five minutes of tidying after an
anchor, growing by area. File:
[`content/journeys/tidy-a-little.json`](../content/journeys/tidy-a-little.json).
Evidence, with what was checked where:
[tidying](research.md#tidying-evidence-for-the-journey-tidy-a-little).

## Stages

All stages daily; anchor "dinner".

| Stage       | Plan ("After dinner, I will …")                                              | Condition    |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| `one-thing` | put one thing back where it belongs                                          | 5 of 7 days  |
| `table`     | tidy the table for five minutes, with a timer                                | 5 of 7 days  |
| `counter`   | tidy the kitchen counter after the table, five minutes in all                | 5 of 7 days  |
| `one-more`  | tidy one more spot of my choice after table and counter, five minutes in all | 8 of 10 days |

The time stays at five minutes from the second stage on; what grows is
the area. That works because a spot that is cleared most days takes
less time, so the same five minutes reach further.

The journey builds a **Home** ([game rules](game-rules.md) §7) with
bunting, the ladder's default: it is about the place one lives in. Four
stages use all five versions. Their names in the journey's words: A
fire to come home to, A tent with everything in reach, A hut with a
clear table, A cottage in good order, A house where things have a
place.

## How it follows the evidence and the design rules

- **Start tiny** (rule 1): one thing put back takes seconds, also with
  guests or after a long day. It stays the fallback (rule 11); the
  fourth nugget says so as an if-then plan.
- **One anchor, right after a routine** (rule 2): the plan follows
  dinner in every stage. In one small study a new action placed after an
  existing routine tended to become a stronger habit than one placed
  before it[^judah2013].
- **A clear end:** the timer ends the task whatever is left (second
  nugget). No study of time-limited tidying was found; the five minutes
  are a design choice that keeps the step small.
- **No well-being promise** (rule 26, guide rule 9): that a cluttered
  home goes with stress is correlational[^saxbe2010], and lab studies of
  orderly rooms cut both ways[^vohs2013]. The texts speak of things
  having a place and of a few clear spots, not of a clean home, mood or
  stress; the reason is the user's own (`why`, rule 5).
- **The user's choice** (rule 15): the last area is "one more spot of my
  choice"; texts say "you could".
- **Nuggets** (rule 22): choosing the one thing while still at the
  table; a timer where one eats and stopping when it rings; a home close
  by for things that keep landing on the counter; an if-then plan for a
  full evening.
- **No promised number of days** (rule 4), no quotes (rule 5), no
  opponents, points or levels (rule 17); story texts speak of pieces
  that stand and grow without naming what is built (rule 20).
- The part that differs comes first in each action ("tidy the table",
  "tidy the kitchen counter", "tidy one more spot").

## Later

Other anchors (after breakfast, on coming home) and a weekly, larger
round are possible journeys of their own; this one keeps one anchor and
one length.
