# Journey: Evening wind-down

Issue #37. The owner chose the topic: screen off and one small fixed
routine before bed, growing towards a steady bedtime. File:
[`content/journeys/evening-wind-down.json`](../content/journeys/evening-wind-down.json).
Evidence, with what was checked where:
[evening wind-down](research.md#evening-wind-down-evidence-for-the-journey-evening-wind-down).

## Stages

All stages daily; anchor "brushing my teeth in the evening".

| Stage        | Plan ("After brushing my teeth in the evening, I will …")                          | Condition    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| `charger`    | put my phone on its charger for the night (away from my bed)                       | 5 of 7 days  |
| `calm-2`     | do one calm thing for two minutes, with my phone on its charger                    | 5 of 7 days  |
| `calm-10`    | give ten minutes to my calm thing, with my phone on its charger                    | 5 of 7 days  |
| `lights-out` | turn the light out at about my chosen time, after the charger and ten calm minutes | 8 of 10 days |

The journey builds a **Crossing** ([game rules](game-rules.md) §7) with
lanterns, the ladder's default: an evening routine is a way over from
the day into the night. Four stages use all five versions. Their names
in the journey's words: Stepping stones at dusk, A log over the quiet
water, A plank bridge into the evening, A stone bridge at nightfall, A
covered bridge for the night. Home is what the morning journey builds.

## How it follows the evidence and the design rules

- **Start tiny** (rule 1): putting the phone on its charger takes
  seconds on the latest night; every later stage contains it, so it is
  the fallback (rule 11), and the third nugget says so as an if-then
  plan.
- **One anchor** (rule 2): brushing teeth is a routine most people have
  right before bed, also away from home. A clock time was the other
  candidate; it does not work on a late evening, so the steady time
  comes last and as "about my chosen time".
- **No sleep promise** (rule 26, guide rule 9): for adults, an expert
  panel did not reach consensus that screen use before bed impairs
  sleep[^hartstein2024]; the one trial of putting the phone away is a
  pilot with 38 students and self-reported sleep[^he2020]. The texts
  therefore speak of a calmer end of the day and give the phone a place;
  they do not mention light, melatonin, sleep quality or energy.
- **The calm thing is the user's choice** (rule 15): the examples in the
  second nugget are a few pages of a book[^finucane2021], a stretch, or
  writing down tomorrow's list[^scullin2018]; both studies are short and
  are not quoted as results.
- **About the same time, not a prescribed hour:** regular sleep timing
  goes with lower mortality in a large cohort[^windred2024], but that is
  observational, and assigning regular schedules to adults without sleep
  problems has shown limited effects[^irish2015]. The last stage is a
  shape for the evening that the user sets; no hour and no sleep
  duration is named.
- **A plan instead of willpower:** going to bed later than intended goes
  with lower self-regulation[^kroese2014]; the journey answers with the
  plan sentence and a quiet alarm before the anchor (fourth nugget).
- **Nuggets** (rule 22): a fixed spot for the charger and a book where
  the phone lay; choosing the calm thing; an if-then plan for a late
  night; a quiet alarm, and that a late evening changes nothing.
- **No promised number of days** (rule 4), no quotes (rule 5), no
  opponents, points or levels (rule 17); story texts speak of pieces
  that are laid and grow without naming what is built (rule 20).
- The part that differs comes first in each action ("do one calm thing
  for two minutes", "give ten minutes", "turn the light out").

## Not for insomnia

The journey is a routine, not a treatment. Sleep hygiene education alone
is ineffective against insomnia[^irish2015]; the texts make no such
claim.
